# Claims Register
## Every load-bearing number in the corpus, and what it actually rests on

*Created August 2026. Structure adapted from a claim audit generated in Gemini NotebookLM — the method was better than anything the project had. **This is a standing instrument, not a one-off.** Anything that goes into a deck, a page or a conversation should be checked here first.*

---

## How to use it

**Four tiers.** Tier 1 and 2 can be stated plainly. Tier 3 needs hedging. **Tier 4 must never appear as fact.**

| Tier | Meaning | How to speak about it |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · External** | Verifiable against a government or primary source | State it, cite it |
| **2 · Project material** | Internally consistent and documented, but only in our own files | State it as our figure or our target |
| **3 · Reasonable interpretation** | A defensible inference from something real | Hedge: *"we estimate"*, *"on the assumption that"* |
| **4 · Unverified assumption** | Invented, modelled, or illustrative | **Say so, every time** |

**The safer-wording column is the point.** *"The 50-family threshold"* invites a challenge you cannot answer. *"A proposed target of 50 households to reach initial social density"* survives the same conversation. The claim is unchanged; only the exposure differs.

## Standing rules

1. **Nothing at Tier 4 enters a deck, a page or a conversation without the word "assumed", "modelled" or "illustrative" attached.**
2. **The prototypes already do this correctly.** Every artifact carries a speculative-material footer. Keep that. It is the project's main credibility asset.
3. **When a claim is verified, move it up a tier here and record the source.** This file is where evidence accumulates.
4. **The three to verify first**, because the most argument rests on them: 協力隊 80%/50% · renovation labour share · Bungotakada decline rate.

## Where this came from

The audit structure was generated by Gemini NotebookLM reading the corpus, and it caught things this project had not caught about itself — most usefully, that the time dividend is presented as an assumption in one section and as fact in every other.

**What it could not know:** it read the files as they stood before the August framing correction, so it authoritatively restates the retired *"coordination failure / everyone is waiting on everyone"* diagnosis, treats the resolved Famichi/Musubu naming as an open contradiction (it was reading superseded root-directory duplicates), and re-asserts the 50-family standard and a 3–5 city parallel launch.

**The lesson is general and worth keeping:** a summarising tool inherits a corpus's confidence without inheriting its caveats, except exactly where you ask it to audit. Which is a good argument for auditing often — and for this file existing.
