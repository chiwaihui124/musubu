# Musubu — Fukuoka Pitch
## Bringing a project to a team, not a business plan to investors

*Written August 2026. Outward-facing. Everything else in this repository is internal strategy; this is the thing that gets sent.*

---

## The Framing That Makes This Work

**This is not a fundraise. It is a contribution.**

> *"Here is a fully-formed project. I am not asking you to fund me to run it. I am asking whether you want to build it — and whether there's a seat for me on the team."*

That framing is unusual, disarming, and far easier to say yes to than a solo-founder raise. It also happens to be true, which is why it will hold up under questioning.

It removes the three objections that would otherwise end the conversation immediately:
- *Can this person execute in Japan?* — Not being claimed.
- *Do they speak Japanese?* — Not being claimed.
- *Are they asking for a lot of money on a long timeline?* — No.

What is being offered is the part that's genuinely hard to buy: **eighteen months of connected strategic thinking, an original thesis, and a working prototype**, handed over by someone who wants to help build it rather than own it.

## The One-Paragraph Version

> Japan's rural depopulation is usually treated as a preference problem — people don't want to live in the countryside. It isn't. **Tokyo is simply the only option with a known shape.** People can picture it; they cannot picture a town they have never lived in, and cannot tell from outside whether there is work, whether it is declining, or whether they would be the only one there. A place nobody can evaluate is not an option — it is a guess, so choosing Tokyo is rational even for people who would rather not. Musubu applies a threshold mechanic — the Kickstarter model — to migration itself: families, employers, towns and builders declare conditional commitments that all resolve at the same moment. **What that adds is not persuasion. It is specification** — who is coming, what the work pays, when it happens, and what occurs if it doesn't fill. The choice was never Tokyo versus the countryside. It was Tokyo versus a question mark.

## The Insight, in Two Moves

**1. Relocation is a state, not a trait.**
People don't move because they prefer the countryside. They move because their life is already in motion — a first child, a breakup, burnout, kids leaving, retirement. At those moments the costs of moving are already being paid for other reasons. You aren't persuading anyone to uproot; you're catching people already mid-air and offering a better landing. That makes the addressable population enormous and *annually renewing*.

**2. What Musubu sells cannot be bought anywhere else: simultaneous arrival.**
The last time most adults joined a group where everyone arrived at once and nobody was already inside was university. That's why those years are the most friendship-dense of most people's lives — not youth, but the absence of an established group to break into. Every adult move after 22 is the opposite, which is why relocation is lonely and why "move to the countryside" fails for individuals who genuinely wanted it.

**Musubu manufactures freshman year on demand, for adults, at any life stage.** It is the one good that cannot exist without coordination — which is precisely why nobody sells it.

## Why Now

The coordination mechanic is old technology. Escrow and progress bars are 2009.

What was missing is the layer underneath: **the ability to understand thousands of people well enough to propose a specific life to each of them.** Surveys could only ever return what their authors already imagined, because every question had to be pre-coded. That ceiling is gone. Open questions are now askable at national scale, which means the blocker taxonomy can *emerge from what people actually say* rather than being invented in advance.

It also means a very small team can do what previously needed a large one — which is the operative fact for anyone deciding whether to start this.

## Why Would Yours Work When a Decade of Policy Did Not

*Added September 2026, from `01_Foundation/musubu_coordination_theory.md` §6. This is the first question an informed reader asks, and it needs a one-paragraph answer.*

Not because the idea is cleverer. Because it pulls a different lever. Systems thinking ranks interventions from weakest to strongest: adjusting numbers (subsidies, grants, standards) is the weakest; changing what information exists and what rules apply is several rungs higher. A decade of 地方創生 worked almost entirely at the weakest level: a ¥1,000,000 relocation grant, twelve hundred roadside stations, renovated facilities. Musubu changes what people can see (who else is coming, what stops people, what lapsed) and one rule (nothing binds until enough others bind). Those are information and rule levers, they are far cheaper than grant programmes, and the record shows the lower levers have already been pulled hard.

## Why Kyushu, and Why Fukuoka Specifically

- **The pilot is on the same island.** Bungotakada is ~2 hours away. This is a Kyushu problem with a Kyushu test site.
- **Fukuoka is Japan's startup city by explicit policy** — a National Strategic Special Zone for startups, with a startup visa scheme it pioneered.
- **Fukuoka Growth Next occupies a converted former elementary school.** A project about turning closed buildings back into living ones, pitched inside a startup hub that is itself a revived closed school, is not a coincidence worth wasting.
- **Regional revitalization is a funded national priority**, not a niche cause.

→ *Verify current Fukuoka startup-visa terms and Growth Next status via `japanese-research` before the meeting. These are well-established but should not be asserted from memory in a room.*

## How Musubu Earns

**This was a genuine gap in the existing material and needs to be crisp, because it is the first question.**
The economics documents model each *town's* viability in detail — and never model the company's.

### Near-term — government-funded, credible, unglamorous

| Line | Buyer | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Coordination contract per pilot** | Municipality | Towns already hold revitalization budget and currently spend it on brochures and individual relocation grants with poor retention |
| **Platform / intelligence licence** | Municipality | The Town Intelligence Dashboard is market research towns have never had |
| **Retention-linked placement fee** | Municipality / prefecture | Paid per household *retained at 12 months*, not per household moved — aligns the incentive with the thing that actually matters |

### Medium-term

- **Employer coordination fees** — companies relocating, project-cohort staffing
- **Housing coordination margin** — renovation project management
- **Operator share** — if Musubu co-runs housing with a partner such as Oak House

### The ceiling

**The intent-and-blocker dataset.** Every ministry has already heard that people would consider leaving Tokyo; that is not new and it does not move budget. What does not exist anywhere is a ranked, segmented, annually-refreshed national map of *what precisely stops them* — and policy is written against blockers, not desires. That dataset is what turns Musubu from a company into infrastructure the state has a reason to rely on.

### And the honest de-risking note

A meaningful share of early cost is **plausibly grant-funded** — the project has a legitimate simultaneous claim on reskilling, regional revitalization, *and* cultural preservation budgets, because it genuinely does all three. That is unusual, and for an investor it means less dilution and a longer runway than the sector norm.

## What Would Have to Happen First

**These are team activities, not solo ones.** That is precisely the point being made by bringing this to people rather than attempting it alone.

1. **Get a renovation labour cost from a Bungotakada contractor.** One conversation, in Japanese. It gates the sweat-equity model, the housing economics, and the threshold size — and it is currently an estimate sitting inside an otherwise evidenced argument.
2. **Native review and live testing of the Japanese intake instrument**, then 8–10 real respondents.
3. **First contact with Bungotakada city** — the 成蹊幼稚園 status question is a genuine reason to call, and doubles as an opening.
4. **Approach Oak House** with deal shape B, which requires no capital from either side.

Each needs Japanese fluency, local presence, or institutional standing. **None of them are things one non-Japanese-speaking individual should be attempting**, and pretending otherwise would be the fastest way to discredit the whole thing.
