**The Concept:**
- Preserving and celebrating Japan's mid-20th century (1950s-1980s)
- Period of rapid economic growth, modernization, family values
- Nostalgic appeal to elderly (lived it), middle-aged (childhood memories), young (curiosity)

**Architectural Preservation:**
- Restored 1950s-80s shopfronts and signage
- Period-appropriate building facades
- Vintage streetscapes (Instagram-worthy, film locations)

**Cultural Experiences:**

- Period fashion and music

**Festival Programming:**

## Risk Analysis & Mitigation

### HIGH-PRIORITY RISKS

**Risk 1: Commitment Threshold Not Met**

**Impact:** HIGH - Can't launch if <50 families commit  
**Probability:** MEDIUM - Depends on marketing effectiveness

**Mitigation:**
1. **Extensive pre-launch marketing**
   - National media campaign
   - Clear value proposition
   - Compelling narrative (Showa culture + family values)
   - Bungotakada's existing success stories

2. **Lower threshold if needed**
   - Could start with 30-40 families if market shows
   - Adjust economic model accordingly
   - Still viable at smaller scale

3. **Extended commitment period**
   - Allow more time if close but not quite there
   - Don't rush to failure
   - Build momentum gradually

4. **Risk: Low but manageable through strong marketing**

**Risk 2: Community Resistance**

**Impact:** HIGH - Could derail project even with government support  
**Probability:** LOW - But must monitor

**Mitigation:**
1. **Early community engagement**
   - Town halls BEFORE arrival
   - Address concerns proactively
   - Show economic benefits clearly

2. **Gradual integration**
   - Families arrive over weeks, not all at once
   - Overwhelming = resentment
   - Phased approach = absorption

3. **Cultural sensitivity training**
   - Musubu families learn local customs
   - Respect for existing community
   - Participate, don't dominate

4. **Local champions**
   - Identify supporters in community
   - They become advocates
   - Peer-to-peer persuasion more effective

**Risk 3: Economic Model Fails**

**Impact:** CATASTROPHIC - Families can't support themselves  
**Probability:** LOW - Eldercare demand guaranteed

**Mitigation:**
1. **Multiple facility partnerships**
   - Not dependent on single employer
   - 3-5 facilities minimum
   - Spread risk

2. **Written employment commitments**
   - Legally binding if possible
   - At minimum: MOU with penalties
   - Verified job availability

3. **Diverse income streams**
   - Not everyone in eldercare
   - Remote workers (20%)
   - Entrepreneurs (10%)
   - Traditional jobs (10%)

4. **Emergency fund**
   - Community savings for unemployment
   - 6 months reserve
   - Insurance against failure

**Impact:** MEDIUM - Families can't move in on time  
**Probability:** MEDIUM - Construction often delayed

1. **Buffer time in schedule**
   - Plan 12 months, allow 15
   - Don't overpromise timeline
   - Start renovations early

2. **Backup housing**
   - Temporary rentals if needed
   - Families can move, wait for final houses
   - Not ideal but workable

3. **Multiple renovation teams**
   - Don't depend on single contractor
   - Parallel work reduces bottleneck

**Risk 5: Government Subsidy Cuts**

**Impact:** MEDIUM - Increases family financial burden  
**Probability:** LOW - But possible

**Mitigation:**
1. **Diversified funding**
   - Multiple subsidy programs
   - Not reliant on single source
   - Private investment backup

2. **Conservative budgeting**
   - Assume 50% subsidy, not 70%
   - Pleasant surprise if more
   - Can handle if less

3. **Family reserves**
   - Families should have 6 months savings
   - Screen for financial stability
   - Not "rescue the desperate"
