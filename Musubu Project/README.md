# Musubu Project — Master Index

> **One-line summary:** Musubu is a concept for coordinated relocation to Japan's emptying towns. A town says what it is short of; people commit conditionally; the number is public; if it fills, everyone arrives in the same season, and if it does not, nobody has moved anywhere. What coordination adds is specification, not persuasion: a town you have never lived in becomes something you can weigh against Tokyo on the same terms.

> **If you are a person opening this folder for the first time, read `00_START_HERE.md`.** This README is the full index and was written for people already working inside the project.

---

> **Before any figure goes into a deck, a page or a conversation, check `CLAIMS_REGISTER.md`.** It tiers every load-bearing number in this corpus by what it actually rests on, and gives safer wording for the ones that cannot carry weight. Several claims used confidently across these documents are Tier 4 — modelled or invented — including the 25–35 hour time dividend and the renovation labour share.

> **To find something to work on, open `OPEN_THREADS.md`.** Every unresolved question in the corpus, in one list, tagged by what would actually close it. Twenty-two are decisions somebody could make today; nine need a Japanese speaker; twelve cannot move until something is running.

## Document Summaries

### 01_Foundation — The "Why"

**`musubu_project_overview.md`** → **archived September 2026** in `_archive/superseded-2026-09/`
The original spine, and for a year the canonical starting point. Both of its central claims were corrected in August 2026 by `musubu_transition_thesis.md` (wanting rural life is a state, not a trait; the problem is illegibility, not a standoff) and its six fixed town types were replaced by `musubu_archetype_axes.md`. It was archived because a stranger opening this folder met the retired diagnosis before the current one. Start with `musubu_transition_thesis.md` instead.

**`musubu_transition_thesis.md`** ★ *August 2026*

- **Trait → State correction** — People don't relocate because they prefer the countryside; they relocate because their life is already in motion. Wanting rural life is not a stable trait to be found, it's a transient state to be caught.
- **Transition points** — The eleven life hinges (first child, breakup, burnout, kids leaving, retirement, bereavement…) where the costs of moving are already being paid for other reasons
- **Three consequences** — Addressable population is huge and renews annually; you aren't persuading anyone to uproot; **timing beats targeting**
- **The core offer: simultaneous arrival** — Nobody is already inside. The manufactured freshman-year condition, for adults, at any life stage. A category with no competitor because it cannot exist without coordination.
- **Volume vs. repetition** — Cities give you volume, towns give you repetition, friendship runs on repetition. The structural argument that doesn't rely on nostalgia.
- **The Four Social Engines** — Pre-arrival · contact you don't have to be brave for · side-by-side not face-to-face · purpose and status. Romance needs no fifth engine.
- **Supporting offers** — The portfolio day, composition as *permission* (not just fit), the intergenerational match, time as enabling condition rather than parallel benefit
- **Downstream change table** — What this forces in survey, framing, archetypes, messaging, portal
→ *Connects to: living_profile (intake), archetype_axes (structure), portal_feed_spec (evidence), time_revolution (substrate)*

**`musubu_voice_and_posture.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
The register everything else should be delivered in, in three words: **coordination, hope, let's build it together.** Coordination is the argument; hope is the payload — not a mood but the defensible claim that this is *possible*, earned by the mechanism being ordinary rather than magical; and the invitation positions the reader as a participant rather than a customer, which is the only honest framing for a product whose value comes entirely from other people also saying yes. Establishes the **living-thing posture**: nothing is set in stone, everything is built to adapt, and when a question like *who may vote on this* arises the right move is usually to make the permission configurable rather than resolve the policy first. Introduces a distinction worth holding deliberately — **vision artifacts vs. day-one artifacts**. Every prototype built so far is the former; conflating them oversells to a municipality and undersells to an investor, and **no day-one set exists yet**, which it should before any real conversation with a town, because the first thing a town official asks is what happens in month one. Closes with the tone rules: say the difficult number first · specific beats aspirational · never sell the outcome, build the conditions · show what did not work · never let an ask read as a filter.
→ *Connects to: everything outward-facing — fukuoka_pitch, portal_taxonomy, 2032_vision, intake_instrument, future_development_notes (framing caution)*

**`musubu_life_stage_doors.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
Corrects a real skew: everything the project had built pointed at families and the elderly, while **the twenty-somethings are the strongest use case in the model and nothing was built for them.** The argument for making the first cohort young: they are the cheapest to move (the blocker taxonomy is nearly empty for a 23-year-old, which means coordination is almost the *only* thing stopping them), the highest-leverage to keep (forty years, the children, the businesses), the group for whom simultaneous arrival is a memory rather than an abstraction — and the only segment where the offer is *genuinely competitive*, since what Tokyo gives a 23-year-old is expensive rent, non-regular work and a social life that costs money. Defines **four doors** — one mechanism, four completely different pitches: 20s (*not retirement — work, a trade, twenty people your age, a fifth of the cost*), 30s (*not starting over — skills somewhere they count for more*), 50s (*a town that tells you before you sign whether the customers exist*), and retired (*not somewhere to be looked after, somewhere to be needed* — the framing every rural pitch to older people gets wrong, since what people miss after retiring is being needed, which a shrinking town has in surplus). Records the prototype's design decisions: the competitor is not city-vs-country but *this vs. moving to Tokyo for a job you don't care about*; lead with the full money comparison (¥21,000 left over versus ¥103,000); state six honest downsides at the same size as the upsides, because **the status anxiety — being read as having failed — is the real one and no economic argument touches it**; and make leaving designed-in. Connects to **intentional neighbourhoods**: districts with soft edges, planned for balance rather than accreting chaotically, which is how four cohorts can share one small town. Closes by flagging **immigration** as the genuinely unexamined question cutting across all four doors — coordinated arrival is plausibly worth *more* to a foreign arrival than a Japanese one.
→ *Connects to: transition_thesis (this is it operationalised), density_ladder (intentional neighbourhoods; what holds a 23-year-old at 27), intake_instrument (the blocker taxonomy is near-empty at 23), eldercare_wellness_town (the immigration thread), voice_and_posture (honest downsides)*

**`musubu_coordination_theory.md`** ★ *(NEW, September 2026, drafted by Claude at Wai's request)*
The coordination idea pushed past where the corpus had taken it, using eight disciplines. **Places are illegible, and so are people**: preference falsification and pluralistic ignorance (Kuran), in Japanese 本音/建前 and 出る杭は打たれる, mean nobody can see what anyone else privately wants, and a conditional pledge with a public count is the known remedy, a *mirror* rather than a progress bar. Five new mechanics follow: **self-set thresholds and the cohort fixpoint** (Granovetter, elicited directly, with a worked example); **the refund bonus** (Tabarrok's dominant assurance contract, which makes the first pledge rational before anyone else has pledged); **paired pledges and the pledge graph** (Centola's complex contagion: moving spreads through clusters, so make the pair the unit); **the closing day as a common-knowledge event** (Chwe); and **the conflict path and graduated response**, the two Ostrom principles the corpus was missing. Meadows' leverage points supply the one-paragraph answer to "why would yours work": a decade of policy pulled the weakest lever and this pulls a stronger one. Two verified Japanese precedents: **ふれあい切符**, 結 with a ledger that ran for thirty years and was hollowed out by a state cash channel; and **安野貴博 / チームみらい**, broad listening with a seat in the Diet since July 2025. Closes with the national version stated as carefully as the literature allows: a nation changes trajectory when a hidden majority discovers itself, the instrument for that is a mirror, and the honesty rules are what make it a mirror rather than a megaphone. Every section carries a confidence label; the last section is where it could be wrong.
→ *Connects to: transition_thesis (extended), mechanics_registry (#32–35 added, #30 extended), demand_test (Q06 added), precedent_research (§5c, §5d added), voice_and_posture (vocabulary), portal_taxonomy §3, fukuoka_pitch, CLAIMS_REGISTER (new rows)*

**`musubu_time_revolution.md`**
The hidden value proposition. Goes beyond cost savings (¥1–2M/year) to argue that the most powerful offer is *time* — Musubu gives people their lives back. Quantifies Tokyo's time poverty crisis (40+ hours/week lost to logistics) and shows how rural coordinated living restores 25–35 hours/week. Argues that time affluence is both the deepest motivator and, paradoxically, leads to higher productivity.
→ *Connects to: platform_vision (messaging), national_survey (motivational questions), future_development_notes (emotional resonance)*

**`musubu_platform_intelligence.md`** *(NEW, April 2026)*
The data and intelligence layer that makes the platform smarter over time. Inspired by Oak House resident composition charts, this describes how Musubu surfaces community vibe through two layers: town's declared direction + revealed interest signals. Defines the Town Intelligence Dashboard (demand overview, life stage segments, priorities/dealbreakers, direction-setting). Shows how survey data becomes the most valuable market research tool towns have ever had. Introduces four data layers (intent → behavior → commitments → post-move) and the "what made people leap" intelligence that feeds back to towns as conversion optimization. Core strategic insight: as behavioral data accumulates, the platform becomes genuinely predictive national coordination infrastructure, not just a migration website.
→ *Connects to: platform_vision_v2 (core feature), survey_strategy (survey as intelligence product), platform_roadmap (MVP vs Phase 2 features)*

**`musubu_coordination_architecture.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
Four structural changes, ordered by how much they change the product. **(1) The threshold is not a families-only mechanic** — it works on industrial capital ("we release the capex when 70 committed workers exist"), municipal budget, developer builds, school enrolment, and worker cohorts. Musubu is a general-purpose conditional commitment engine that happens to point at migration. **(2) The host doesn't have to be a town** — a district inside an existing city runs the same mechanic and removes the project's hardest dependency, cutting time-to-first-cohort from 12–18 months to a plausible 4–6. Includes the "daylight district" concept. **(3) Cohorts can circulate** — project-based temporary migration (the nine-month welder) turns a set of towns into a labour network with genuine network effects, and answers the seasonal-opportunity question left open in `business_coordination`. **(4) Cohorts can be recruited pre-assembled** — whole-company relocation, university labs, parent associations, fan communities. Closes with the momentum section: why continuous trickle fizzles and all-or-nothing doesn't, the four momentum rules (run the demand board before any town is signed; size the first threshold to be *finishable*), and a cheapest-path-to-proof sequence that defers municipal partnership, capital, and akiya renovation until after first proof.
→ *Connects to: business_coordination (extends the Kickstarter mechanic), pilot_scaling_strategy (revises sequencing), living_profile (demand board), transition_thesis (who the cohorts are)*

**`musubu_portal_feed_spec.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
Full specification for the live proof-of-life feed — the surface where daily town life is published. **The central argument: the resident utility and the recruitment artifact are the same object.** "Potluck at the town hall, 7pm, come at 6 if you want to help cook" is useful to residents and is simultaneously the only thing that will ever convince a stranger in Osaka, because nobody can picture "strong community" but everyone can picture chopping vegetables with people they haven't met. Defines the three-lane architecture (**Happening / Forming / Life**), twelve post types, the dual resident/prospect view, and the "Be here for this" CTA that pre-fills trial-visit booking with a specific event on a specific date. Includes integrity mechanics that keep it unfakeable (residents-only posting, retrospective attendance, immutable history, empty shown as empty, no engagement optimisation), LINE ingestion as a v1 requirement for Japan, a JS data model matching the existing prototype's conventions, seven derived town-health metrics, design direction, empty-state handling, and a three-phase build plan.
→ *Connects to: famichi-design-spec §2 (absorbs the Social Calendar), platform_intelligence (composition panel), coordination_architecture (Forming lane = thresholds), transition_thesis (evidence for the offer)*

**`musubu_map_spec.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
The other half of the portal — **the feed shows what's happening, the map shows what could.** Renders the threshold mechanic spatially and unifies concepts currently scattered across seven documents: social appetite becomes district character, the arrival curve becomes a time scrubber, business viability thresholds become tiles that unlock other tiles, archetype axes become map filters. Core principle: **willingness is the content, buildings are the substrate** — a normal property map shows what's for sale, this shows *who else wants it*, and if it looks like a real-estate portal with progress bars it's wrong. Defines a seven-layer stack, **the tile as atomic unit** with a full state machine (including `lapsed` shown honestly, because visible failures are what make successes credible) and a seven-element card — including **per-tile composition**, which is more faithful to the Oak House insight than the town-level version, and **what it unlocks**, which makes tile interdependency visible and is the most persuasive element on the card. The **time scrubber** runs −24 to +36 months in two never-blurring modes (committed vs. gathering), carrying mayor, business owner, and family in a single interaction. Claim mechanics split: living tiles are first-come with a **hard-constraints-only** fit check (the platform must never become a bouncer), business slots are proposals weighed with the future customers — advisory, not binding, since popularity isn't viability. Carries one hard rule: **no tile may be placed over an occupied or actively used structure**, and existing residents and businesses render as valued, not as absence. v1 is deliberately small — one illustrated map, ~20 tiles, not GIS, because the interaction is the product and not the cartography.
→ *Connects to: portal_feed_spec (the other half), coordination_architecture (arrival curve, thresholds), business_coordination (viability math), archetype_axes (district character), intake_instrument (social appetite as zoning), ai_enablement (tile proposal and threshold setting)*

**`musubu_portal_taxonomy.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
Organised around one distinction: **a portal is a place you inhabit, an interface is a thing you operate.** Getting it wrong is expensive in both directions — give an employer a portal and nobody visits or maintains it; give a town only an interface and you've reduced a community to a row in someone else's database, which is exactly the posture that makes municipalities refuse. Three portals (member, town, **cohort**) and three interfaces (employer, ministry, build programme), with the edge case that a *forming* business gets a temporary public tile page that disappears when it opens. The **member portal** section covers the two-directional principle (your profile is a filter *and* a vote, made visible by a why-line on every module), **the package as the unit rather than the job** — since relocation friction is overwhelmingly assembly friction, and the "decided at month seven, not now" field removes an irreversibility fear no subsidy touches — **typed obstacles** in three states, including a *policy* state that shows the member their own wall is shared by 412 others, the dashboard reorganising by life state, and tiers that unlock responsibility rather than features. The **town portal** is named as the most under-built surface in the project: its primary job is **outward signal** — a governing body saying publicly *"we want people ready to start a family, we can house you"* — which turns Layer 1 declared direction from something the platform infers into something the town broadcasts and controls, and completes the two-sided threshold by giving towns somewhere to declare their own conditional position. Includes a visitor/resident/office access matrix. The **cohort portal** is new here: thirty households who have committed but not yet moved are a group with no home, and it's where "community before migration" actually happens — with a deliberate lifespan, since a cohort portal that lingers becomes a clique.

**`musubu_ai_enablement.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
The project's honest "why now," for the pitch. **`musubu_density_ladder.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
**Japan offers two settlement options: Tokyo, or a place that is shrinking.** Every rural municipality declines at roughly the same rate regardless of what it is for, so density stopped being a choice and became a binary. The proposal: hold a cluster of settlements at deliberately different sizes and make moving between them cheap — not one town of 22,000 surrounded by four dying villages, but a region that decides what each settlement is *for* and states the size it is holding. Economically grounded, since services turn on at population thresholds (the "Tipping Effect" from `business_coordination`, applied at regional rather than town scale): **five municipalities each declining 8,000 → 5,000 leaves five places that cannot sustain a clinic; the same population held at 610 + 4,800 + 22,810 leaves three that can.** Prototypes the Kunisaki cluster in Ōita — Tashibu, Yamaga, Bungotakada, all within forty minutes — with a logarithmic scale from 300 people to Tokyo whose real subject is **the gap**: between a service town of 30,000 and the metropolitan band, almost nothing in Japan is growing on purpose. Two ideas came out of building it. **Towns should publish a size they are *holding*, not a target they want to exceed** — "held at 4,500–6,000, because above six thousand the walk-everywhere breaks" is more trustworthy than any welcome message. And **the ladder changes the failure mode of rural migration**: someone who finds a village too small currently has one option and it is Tokyo, whereas a ladder makes the failure mode *moving to a slightly larger part of the countryside* — which addresses the reversibility blocker no subsidy can touch, and means the metric that matters is regional retention rather than town retention. Distinguishes itself from コンパクトシティ / 立地適正化 (voluntary not zoned, inter- not intra-municipal, additive not subtractive) because a Japanese planner will raise that immediately.
→ *Connects to: business_coordination (population maths), archetype_axes (size becomes an axis), pilot_scaling_strategy (rethinks "how many towns"), intake_instrument (reversibility), transition_thesis (life-stage maps onto size), portal_taxonomy (a "held at" field)*

**`musubu_living_profile.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
Converts the survey from an **event** into a **standing register**. A survey is a snapshot that starts decaying the moment it closes; a profile is a live state object people update as their life moves — and because relocation happens at life hinges, **the update event *is* the buying signal**. A one-time questionnaire structurally cannot catch a hinge. Defines five question domains of *state* rather than demographics (where you are now · **what's changing** — the domain currently missing entirely · what you'd consider · what you want from life · what you can do). Specifies a **graduated disclosure** privacy model where every field is independently private / aggregate-only / visible, defaulting to aggregate-only, with full anonymity supported — and argues this costs the project nothing, since the unit of value is the aggregate and names only matter at commitment. Introduces the **aggregate mirror**: show people their slice of the total immediately ("4,300 others in your bracket said the same thing"), which inverts the transaction from harvesting to belonging and is the operational form of *making invisible desire visible*. Includes update triggers, what the profile feeds, and required changes to existing survey materials. Now also carries **the Availability Register** — the work-facing face of the profile, and the sharpest way to say what it is: **LinkedIn is a record of accomplishment; this is a register of willingness.** LinkedIn structurally cannot hold *"I'd leave for nine months," "I'd retrain," "I'd take less money for more time," "I'm free from March"* — declaring any of those damages your standing in the job you have. Here they're the point. No new architecture: it's a visibility preset over the graduated disclosure model. And one discipline — **this is not a jobs board**; the matching object is a `project_cohort` tile with a threshold and a deadline, not a posting.

**`musubu_intake_instrument.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
The working question set — what to ask, why, and how to deploy it. Governed by one rule: **every question must change a proposal.** Anything that wouldn't alter which options a person is shown is decoration, and decoration costs completion rate. Specifies the **loop** (six questions → three deliberately varied proposals → *"which is least wrong, and what's wrong with it?"* → six more chosen by what they rejected), because people cannot say what they want but can instantly say what's wrong with something in front of them — making **rejection data** the engine. Defines the first six questions, then a domain bank of eight areas: hard constraints (including *do you drive*, decisive in rural Japan and never asked); a **time-poverty battery** that converts commute, sleep, and claimed evenings into countable hours and mirrors them straight back (*"620 hours a year in transit — 26 days"*), which is the operational form of what `time_revolution` argues but never measures; work and capability; **social appetite**, the biggest gap in the current model and a *zoning* input as much as a matching one; direction and meaning asked through specifics rather than feelings; **blockers and friction**; behavioural proxies (*"have you ever searched for houses in a place you don't live?"*); and the decision unit, including partner state as a readiness ladder rather than a checkbox. Argues that **the blocker data is the fundable asset** — every ministry has heard that people would consider moving, but nobody has a ranked, segmented, national map of exactly what stops them, and policy is written against blockers rather than desires. Closes with the **census sprint**: a five-day synchronous national window with a pre-registration threshold, rather than a rolling survey, because simultaneity is what makes *"I'm not alone"* land — and because the census should demonstrate the project's own thesis before asking anyone to believe it.
→ *Connects to: living_profile (this is its content), survey_strategy (supersedes the 33-question form), transition_thesis (what it's detecting), archetype_axes (what it feeds), coordination_architecture (blocker data → which product to build), government_alignment (the policy product)*

**`musubu_demand_test.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
The smallest thing that converts the project's central assumption into evidence — written because the corpus reached eighteen documents and six prototypes with **zero people surveyed**. Tests one claim: *people will commit conditionally in a way they will not commit individually.* Everything else is downstream of it. The design principle is to test the load-bearing assumption rather than general interest — *"would you consider the countryside?"* has been asked repeatedly and always returns a soft yes that predicts nothing, because interest is not scarce and **willingness to act is**. So the instrument asks the same person about the same imagined town twice, changing one variable: arriving alone, versus arriving with fifty households where nobody moves unless everyone does. **The delta between those answers is the thesis expressed as a statistic**, and it survives the self-selection objection — a respondent predisposed to say yes is predisposed on both questions, which is why the gap is the metric rather than the headline. Eight questions total, including the transition detector and the blocker question. Specifies the mechanics that make it work: publish everything back (the aggregate mirror), a live counter that makes the respondent *experience* the coordination mechanic by participating in it, showing each person their own delta, and handling contrary answers gracefully because a survey that only hears from people it convinces measures nothing. Recommends **not building a survey platform** — commodity form tool for collection, custom page for results, since no form tool publishes results back well. Names the two real blockers: it must run in Japanese, and **distribution is unsolved and is the most likely failure mode**. Pre-commits to how each possible result should be read, so the outcome cannot be interpreted to taste afterwards.

**`musubu_2032_vision.md`** ★ *(NEW, August 2026)*
The success picture, and the reasoning behind it. Four decisions carry it. **Success is deliberately small** — 176 households over six years, under 2% of the town, because a vision showing the town doubling is a fantasy anyone who knows these places would stop reading. **The hero stat is the counterfactual, not growth** — the chart runs what happened against what would have happened anyway at the town's existing decline, because *"the measure of success was never growth; it was the moment the line stopped falling"* — which is also the correct policy metric and should be adopted in the government pitch. **The existing town is rendered quiet and dominates the surface**, and farmland stays farmed, so the map can never read as a takeover or as a startup eating fields. And **failures stay on the map** — three businesses closed, 88% retention not 100%, one cluster that stalled and is still paddy — because a future with no failures in it is marketing, and every other artifact in this project rests on the honest version persuading better. ## Key Themes Across Documents

**The coordination problem** is the central idea — it appears in `project_overview`, both `platform_vision` docs, `business_coordination`, `coop_strategy`, `musubu_survey_strategy`, and `platform_intelligence`. Every solution in every document traces back to this one insight. For elderly relocation, it's explicitly stated in `eldercare_wellness_town`: "loneliness is a geometry problem. Musubu fixes the geometry."

**Time affluence** (`time_revolution`) is the emotional core of the pitch and should inform messaging across surveys, platform copy, and pilot town materials.

**The Kickstarter mechanic** (conditional commitments, visible progress, threshold activation) is described in `project_overview` and `platform_vision` — it's what makes Musubu a platform rather than just a website.

**Community vibe as the real product** appears in `platform_intelligence` (Oak House insight) and `eldercare_wellness_town` (proof-of-life feed). People aren't choosing units or towns—they're choosing social environments. The platform makes this legible.

**Data as infrastructure** is the Phase 2+ story. `platform_intelligence` + `platform_roadmap` frame the national coordination argument: as behavioral data accumulates, Musubu becomes harder to replicate and justifies government/company reliance.

**Multicultural integration as structural necessity** is articulated most clearly in `eldercare_wellness_town`: caretaker community isn't a side effect—it's central to economic viability and retention. This pattern will repeat across other archetypes.

**Transition state, not preference** (`transition_thesis`, August 2026) is the revised core insight. People don't relocate because they prefer the countryside — they relocate because their life is already in motion. This reframes the addressable population from a small fixed pool into an annually renewing river, and makes *timing* more important than targeting. It is the reason the survey has to become a living profile.

**Simultaneous arrival is the product** (`transition_thesis`). The thing Musubu sells that nothing else can is a group of people who all arrive at once, where nobody is already inside. It's the university-dorm condition, manufactured on demand, for adults. It cannot be bought any other way because it cannot exist without coordination.

**The feed is the proof** (`portal_feed_spec`). The resident utility and the recruitment artifact are the same object, and it can't be faked — a town with nothing happening shows a feed with nothing on it. This is the honest version of everything `survey_strategy` and the design spec's "proof of life over statistics" principle were reaching for.

**The map is the future you can cause** (`map_spec`). A pre-build tile isn't inventory — clicking it isn't shopping, it's voting with your own life. Where the feed shows what's happening, the map shows what could, and together they are the portal. Willingness is the content; the buildings are only the substrate.

**It is not a standoff, it is illegibility** (`transition_thesis`, August 2026 — **supersedes the coordination-failure framing used across the corpus**). Nobody is waiting to see whether others will go. Tokyo is simply the only option with a known shape, and a town you have never lived in cannot be assessed from outside — so it isn't an option, it's a guess. **What coordination adds is specification, not persuasion.** This requires no claim about mass psychology, isn't anti-Tokyo, and points straight at the product: the feed, the composition display, the arrival curve and the published thresholds *are* the solution rather than features of it. The line: *the choice was never Tokyo versus the countryside, it was Tokyo versus a question mark.*

**Coordination generalises** (`coordination_architecture`). The threshold isn't a families mechanic. Every party — employers, developers, municipalities, schools, worker cohorts — holds an "I will if they will" position. **The standoff description is accurate here, for institutions**, even though it is wrong for individuals. And it runs both ways: a builder converting for sixty is making a blind bet on demand, while a hundred households cannot commit to a building that doesn't exist — **the supply is the demand and the demand is the supply**, and the threshold is where both are allowed to say so before either has to act.

**Blockers are the product** (`intake_instrument`). Demand data is not new and does not move budget — every ministry has already heard that people would consider leaving Tokyo. A ranked, segmented, national map of *what precisely stops them* does not exist, and policy is written against blockers rather than desires. This is the asset that turns Musubu from a company with a good idea into infrastructure the state has reason to rely on. It also tells the project which product to build: if uncertainty rather than money is the binding constraint, the answer is trajectory visibility, not larger subsidies.

**Make time countable** (`intake_instrument`, `time_revolution`). People grasp money more soundly than time, so the fix is to convert time into a number as concrete as income and show it back — hours per year in transit, unclaimed waking hours per month. It's arithmetic, not persuasion, which is exactly why it works.

**Why now** (`ai_enablement`). The coordination mechanic is old technology; the layer underneath it isn't. Being able to read fifty thousand people's own words and propose a specific life to each of them is what makes the loop, the emergent blocker taxonomy, and the matching engine possible at all. This is the project's honest answer to why it's buildable today and wasn't a decade ago.

**Each cohort trains the next cohort's builders** (`build_partnership_model`, `living_profile`). The build programme creates the skills the availability register then matches, so the circulating-labour layer generates its own supply instead of waiting for skilled people to appear. It is the first genuinely self-feeding loop in the model — and the reason the renovation programme is a recruitment engine rather than a cost centre.
