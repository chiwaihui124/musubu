# Musubu Akiya Strategy
## Transforming Vacant Homes into Community Assets

---

## Overview

Japan has 8-10 million vacant homes (akiyas) concentrated in rural areas - the exact locations where Musubu operates. Rather than viewing these as obstacles, we transform them into strategic assets that create jobs, preserve culture, build skills, and solve the housing crisis.

**Core Insight:** Akiyas aren't just "cheap housing" - they're a multi-layered economic and cultural opportunity that makes the entire Musubu model more viable.

---

## The Akiya Problem & Opportunity

### Current Situation

**The Crisis:**
- 8-10 million vacant homes across Japan
- Concentrated in rural areas with declining populations
- Many free or near-free (some towns pay to take them)
- Decay creates blight, reduces property values
- Tax burden on municipalities
- Cultural heritage deteriorating

**The Opportunity:**
- Traditional construction (wood, tile, craftsmanship)
- Located exactly where Musubu operates
- Towns desperate for solutions
- Government subsidies available
- Master craftspeople eager to pass on skills before retirement
- Young people increasingly interested in blue-collar/skilled trades

### Market Trends Supporting This Strategy

**Shift Toward Blue-Collar Work:**
Recent trends show Japanese young people increasingly choosing skilled trades over traditional white-collar careers:
- Seeking work-life balance (trades often better hours than corporate)
- Tangible results and satisfaction from hands-on work
- Good income potential without university debt
- Growing respect for craftspeople
- Reaction against "black companies" and overtime culture

**This trend makes akiya renovation particularly timely - we're riding a cultural shift.**

## Three Strategic Uses

### 1. Apprenticeship Economy: Training the Next Generation

**The Model:**
- 10-15 akiyas become training projects
- Partner with master carpenters (aging, eager to pass on skills)
- 12-month apprenticeship program
- Participants learn while renovating actual buildings
- Government vocational training subsidies
- Nationally recognized carpentry certification

**Who This Attracts:**
- Young people wanting skilled trades (growing demographic)
- Career changers from corporate burnout
- People who can't afford traditional apprenticeships
- International craft enthusiasts
- People wanting portable, valuable skills

**Training Structure:**
- Months 1-3: Classroom (theory, tools, safety, blueprint reading)
- Months 4-12: Hands-on (working on akiya renovations)
- 5 days/week practical work
- Stipend: ¥200K/month (government subsidized)

**Curriculum:**
- Traditional Japanese joinery (no nails, interlocking wood)
- Foundation and structural repair
- Roof work (traditional tile and thatch)
- Modern building codes and safety standards
- Historic preservation techniques
- Project estimation and planning
- Customer communication and business basics

**Economic Output:**
- Government training subsidies: ¥200K/month × 15 apprentices = ¥3M/month
- Annual program cost: ¥36M (largely government funded)
- Output: 15 certified carpenters/year + 10-15 renovated akiyas
- Graduate income: ¥280-500K/month depending on experience

---

### 2. Community Infrastructure: Shared Spaces

Rather than expensive new construction, convert akiyas into community facilities.

**Potential Uses:**

**Community Gathering:**
- Community center/town hall (events, meetings, celebrations)
- Library/learning center (books, computers, quiet study)
- Community kitchen/café (shared meals, social hub)
- Performance space (music, theater, art shows)

**Work & Learning:**
- Co-working space (fiber internet, desks, meeting rooms)
- Training center (classrooms, workshop space)
- Maker space (tools, equipment sharing)
- Childcare facility (cheaper than new construction)

**Housing Alternatives:**
- Guest house (for "Live Like Local" trial visits)
- **Co-living spaces** (shared living for singles/couples)
- Artist residency spaces
- Elder co-housing (aging in community)

**Creative & Cultural:**
- Art studios (separated spaces for multiple artists)
- Gallery/exhibition space
- Craft workshop facilities
- Music/recording studios

**Why This Works:**
- **Cost:** Renovation ¥5-15M vs new build ¥30-50M
- **Speed:** Faster than new construction permits
- **Character:** Traditional buildings have charm, identity, history
- **Flexibility:** Can adapt use over time as needs change
- **Cultural preservation:** Maintains town's architectural heritage

**Example Conversion - Tech Training Village:**
- Akiya #1: Co-working space (20 desks, meeting rooms, high-speed internet)
- Akiya #2: Childcare center (large rooms, outdoor play area)
- Akiya #3: Co-living house (6-8 private rooms, shared kitchen/living)
- Akiya #4: Guest house (8 rooms for trial visits)
- Akiya #5: Community kitchen (events, shared meals, cooking classes)

**Total cost:** ¥40M (vs ¥100M+ for equivalent new construction)

### 3. Housing Stock: Phased Renovation Program

**The Housing Challenge:**
- 50 families arriving needs ~50 housing units
- New construction: ¥30-50M per unit = ¥1.5-2.5 BILLION total
- Market rental: Still ¥60-100K/month in rural areas

**The Akiya Solution:**

**Phase 1: Quick Move-Ins (Months 1-6)**
- Identify 10-15 minimally renovated akiyas
- Basic repairs only: roof, plumbing, electrical, safety
- Cost: ¥3-5M per house
- First families arrive, live while other renovations continue
- Some families participate in renovation (sweat equity)

**Phase 2: Community Renovation (Months 6-18)**
- Carpentry apprentices work on projects
- Families help renovate (optional sweat equity program)
- Community work days (build relationships while building homes)
- 20 more homes ready
- Mix of rental and ownership pathways

**Phase 3: Long-term Development (Year 2+)**
- Remaining akiyas renovated as needed
- Continuous improvement and maintenance
- Some become intergenerational housing
- Community skills developed (many can do basic maintenance)

**The Math:**
- 50 akiyas acquired: ¥0-25M (many are free, some towns pay you)
- Renovation average ¥5M each: ¥250M total
- **Total: ¥275M vs ¥1.5-2.5B for new construction**
- **Savings: ¥1.2-2.2 BILLION**

## Co-Living Spaces: A Strategic Housing Option

### Why Co-Living Works for Musubu

**Target Groups:**
- Singles and couples without children
- Apprentices during training period
- Young professionals starting careers
- People testing rural life before committing
- Temporary housing during transition
- Lower-income individuals wanting community

**Benefits:**

**Economic:**
- ¥30-40K/month vs ¥60-80K for private rental
- Utilities included
- No furniture purchase needed
- Lower deposit/key money

**Social:**
- Immediate community and friendships
- Less isolation risk
- Built-in social activities
- Shared meals and experiences
- Natural support network

**Practical:**
- Shared cleaning and maintenance
- Larger common spaces than private apartment
- Shared amenities (better kitchen, living room, etc.)
- Flexible lease terms
- Community management included

**Space Efficiency:**
- One large akiya can house 6-8 people comfortably
- Private bedrooms (8-12 tatami each)
- Shared kitchen, living, bathroom facilities
- Maximizes use of traditional large homes
- Better than leaving empty

### Co-Living Design for Akiya Conversion

**Typical Layout:**
- 6-8 private rooms (sleeping, personal space)
- Large shared kitchen (traditional Japanese irori/hearth if possible)
- 2-3 shared bathrooms/showers
- Common living room (tatami space for gatherings)
- Shared laundry facilities
- Outdoor space (garden, engawa/veranda)
- Optional: shared workspace/study area

**House Rules & Culture:**
- Community agreements (noise, cleaning, guests)
- Shared meal nights (optional, 2-3x/week)
- Cleaning rotation
- Community manager facilitates
- Monthly house meetings

**Renovation Cost:**
- ¥8-12M per house (more than basic, less than full luxury)
- ¥1-1.5M per resident space created
- Payback: 2-3 years at ¥35K/month × 6-8 residents

### Co-Living Models

**Model 1: Young Professional Co-Living**
- Tech workers, remote workers, freelancers
- Private bedrooms, shared workspace
- High-speed internet priority
- Social but independent lifestyle
- ¥35-45K/month per person

**Model 2: Apprentice Housing**
- Carpentry/craft apprentices during training
- Lower cost (¥25-30K/month)
- Built-in community of learners
- Close to training sites
- Some may do house maintenance (sweat equity discount)

**Model 3: Intergenerational Co-Living**
- Mix of young families, singles, elders
- Mutual support (elders help with childcare, youth help with tasks)
- Larger community (12-15 people)
- Modified akiya cluster (2-3 connected buildings)
- Innovative model for Japan

## Mobile Master-Apprentice Teams

### The Concept

Once the first cohort graduates, create **mobile renovation teams** that travel between Musubu towns and other municipalities.

**Why This Works:**

**For Graduates:**
- Business opportunity (contract work)
- Variety and travel (see different parts of Japan)
- Flexibility (not tied to one location)
- Build reputation and portfolio
- Good income (¥400-600K/month as skilled contractors)

**For Musubu:**
- Spreads renovation expertise across towns
- Faster scaling (don't need to train in every town)
- Network effects (knowledge sharing)
- Creates Musubu "brand" for quality work
- Revenue stream (coordination fees)

**For Towns:**
- Access to skilled workers without permanent hiring
- Akiya problem solved efficiently
- Learn from teams, build local capacity
- Part of larger movement (not isolated)

### Program Structure

**Team Composition:**
- 1 Master Carpenter (lead, 10+ years experience)
- 2-3 Journeymen (Musubu graduates, 2-5 years experience)
- 2-4 Apprentices (current students, learning on the job)
- Total: 5-8 person team

**Engagement Model:**
- **6-month contracts** (as you suggested)
- Town commits to renovating X akiyas
- Team moves to town for duration
- Housing provided by town
- Mix of Musubu families and local workers

**Typical 6-Month Project:**
- Month 1: Assessment, planning, materials ordering
- Months 2-5: Renovation work (3-6 akiyas depending on scope)
- Month 6: Final details, local training, handoff

**Economics:**
- Town pays: ¥400K-600K per akiya renovation (below market)
- Team revenue: ¥2-4M per 6-month project
- Split among team members
- Cheaper than hiring separately
- Quality assured (Musubu trained)

### Rotation Benefits

**For Individuals:**
- "I want to spend 6 months in Hokkaido, then 6 months in Kyushu"
- See different parts of Japan while working
- Build diverse portfolio and experience
- Network across multiple towns
- Option to settle in favorite location later

**For Families:**
- If both partners are carpenters, travel together
- If one partner remote worker, location independent anyway
- Children can be homeschooled or transfer schools (shorter commitments)
- Adventurous lifestyle appeals to some families

**For Apprentices:**
- Part of training = working in different towns
- Learn regional variations in building styles
- Build national network
- Decide where to eventually settle

### Integration with Musubu Network

**Central Coordination:**
- Musubu HQ maintains team roster
- Towns submit project requests
- Teams bid or are assigned
- Quality standards maintained
- Dispute resolution
- Knowledge sharing platform

**Quality Assurance:**
- All team leaders Musubu-certified
- Standard processes and techniques
- Regular skill assessments
- Customer satisfaction tracking
- Portfolio documentation

**Knowledge Transfer:**
- Teams train local workers during projects
- Document techniques (videos, manuals)
- Leave town with increased capability
- Some locals join next rotation
- Spreads skills broadly

## Economic Model Analysis

### Carpentry Apprenticeship Program Economics

**Annual Operating Costs:**
- Master carpenter salaries (3): ¥18M
- Program coordinator: ¥5M
- Facilities and equipment: ¥4M
- Materials for training: ¥3M
- Administrative costs: ¥2M
- Marketing/recruitment: ¥2M
- **Total: ¥34M/year**

**Annual Revenue:**
- Government training subsidies (15 students × ¥200K × 12): ¥36M
- Some akiya sales after renovation: ¥5-10M
- Training fees from other municipalities: ¥2-3M
- **Total: ¥43-49M/year**

**Result:** Self-sustaining after year 2

### Akiya Renovation Economics

**Per House Acquisition & Renovation:**
- Acquisition cost: ¥0-500K (many free)
- Basic structural work: ¥2M
- Electrical/plumbing: ¥1.5M
- Finishes and details: ¥1.5M
- **Total: ¥5M average**

**Government Subsidies Available:**
- MLIT Empty House Renovation: ¥1.5M/house
- Local municipality incentive: ¥500K/house
- **Net cost: ¥3M/house**

**If Sold:**
- Renovated akiya value: ¥8-12M
- Profit margin: ¥3-7M
- Can subsidize other community uses

**If Rented:**
- Monthly rent: ¥40-60K
- Annual revenue: ¥480-720K
- ROI: 5-7 years

**If Co-living:**
- 6 residents × ¥35K: ¥210K/month
- Annual revenue: ¥2.5M
- ROI: 2-3 years

### Community Space Conversion Economics

**Example: Co-working Space**
- Akiya acquisition: ¥0-500K
- Renovation for co-working: ¥10M
  - Structural work: ¥3M
  - Internet infrastructure: ¥2M
  - Furniture and equipment: ¥3M
  - Climate control: ¥2M
- **Total: ¥10-10.5M**

**Revenue:**
- 20 desks × ¥25K/month: ¥500K/month
- Meeting room rentals: ¥100K/month
- Events and workshops: ¥50K/month
- **Total: ¥650K/month = ¥7.8M/year**

**ROI:** ~18 months

## Modular Add-On Programs

The beauty of the akiya strategy is its **modularity** - towns can choose which elements to include:

### Core Module (All Towns)
- Basic akiya acquisition and renovation for housing
- 20-30 family homes
- Government subsidies leveraged
- **Required investment:** ¥60-90M

### Apprenticeship Module (Optional)
- Add full carpentry training program
- 10-15 apprentices per cohort
- Master carpenter employment
- Creates jobs while building housing
- **Additional investment:** ¥15M initial, then self-sustaining

### Community Spaces Module (Optional)
- Convert 3-5 akiyas to shared facilities
- Co-working, library, community center, etc.
- Reduces need for new construction
- **Additional investment:** ¥30-50M

### Co-Living Module (Optional)
- Convert 2-4 akiyas to co-living houses
- Housing for 15-30 singles/couples
- Fast ROI (2-3 years)
- **Additional investment:** ¥20-35M

### Mobile Team Module (Year 2+)
- Graduate apprentices form traveling teams
- Serve other towns
- Network effects
- Revenue generating
- **No additional investment, creates income**

### Tourism/Residency Module (Optional)
- Guest houses for visitors
- Artist residencies
- "Learn carpentry" tourism
- Heritage experience stays
- **Additional investment:** ¥15-25M

**Towns customize based on:**
- Available akiya stock
- Population needs
- Economic priorities
- Community interest
- Available funding

## Risk Mitigation

### Structural Risks
- Some akiyas too deteriorated to renovate economically
- **Mitigation:** Thorough assessment before acquisition, maintain 20% buffer in budget

### Skills Gap
- Not enough master carpenters willing to teach
- **Mitigation:** Competitive compensation, flexible schedules, prestige of preserving heritage

### Market Risk
- Renovated homes don't sell or rent
- **Mitigation:** Guaranteed demand from Musubu families, flexible use (convert to co-living or community use)

### Quality Issues
- Apprentice work doesn't meet standards
- **Mitigation:** Master oversight, staged skill development, quality checks before completion

### Regulatory
- Building codes, permits, historic preservation rules
- **Mitigation:** Early engagement with officials, hire expert consultants, work within existing frameworks

## The Vision

**"We're not just moving to rural Japan. We're restoring it - one akiya at a time."**

This strategy transforms the akiya problem into the Musubu advantage:
- Creates jobs (carpentry, hospitality, maintenance)
- Builds skills (portable, valuable, fulfilling)
- Solves housing crisis (affordable, beautiful, cultural)
- Preserves heritage (techniques, buildings, knowledge)
- Enables community (co-living, shared spaces, collective pride)
- Proves scalable (mobile teams, knowledge transfer)
- Tells compelling story (visible transformation, human interest)

Every restored akiya is proof that rural Japan can be renewed. Every certified carpenter is proof that meaningful careers exist outside Tokyo. Every co-living resident is proof that community can be designed. Every mobile team is proof that the model scales.

**The akiya strategy isn't just about buildings. It's about showing that Japan's past and future can work together.**
