# What Success Actually Looks Like
## Reverse-engineering the pilot target from the town's own arithmetic

*Written 9 August 2026. Worked against Okutama as the reference case. Method transfers to any candidate town.*

---

## The method

Don't pick a number that sounds impressive. **Pick the smallest institutional threshold the town is currently below, and make that the target.** Success is then not "N people moved in" — it's *one thing exists that could not otherwise have existed.*

This is the registry's own logic applied to the project's own goals. §R3 rejects population bars because they frame a town as a container. §14 says the only defensible metric is the delta the programme is responsible for. §3 says thresholds are typed by institution. Put together, they give a success definition that is checkable, attributable, and small enough to actually hit.

## The reference case: Okutama

| | |
|---|---|
| Population | **4,409** (1 Jan 2025) |
| Change | **−124 in the year**; −1,072 over ten years (**−19.6%**) |
| Aging rate | 46.9% |
| **Composition of decline** | **In-migration ≈ out-migration. The decline is now almost entirely natural — deaths over births.** |

*Provenance: municipal — 奥多摩町 published population figures and 全国町村会 case study. **Correction: an earlier figure of ~5,200 in this corpus was wrong.** 4,409 is the current number.*

**That composition finding is the important one.** Okutama has already solved the outflow problem — people are not fleeing any more. It is dying of age structure, not of abandonment. Which means:

- The counterfactual is **zero net social gain**. Any sustained positive net in-migration is entirely attributable.
- The problem is not persuasion. It is arithmetic. The town needs bodies of childbearing and working age, and no amount of retention effort produces them.
- **This is the cleanest possible attribution environment for a pilot.** You are not trying to beat an existing trend. You are trying to create one where the current value is zero.

## Level 1 — the standstill number

To hold population flat, Okutama needs to replace **124 people a year**.

| Household mix | Households/year needed |
|---|---|
| Mixed (2.4 people avg) | **≈52** |
| Family-skewed (3.0 avg) | **≈41** |

Allowing for churn — the 協力隊 proxy suggests ~31% eventually leave the region — gross intake needs to run about 1.45× net:

> **≈40–50 households a year net. ≈60–75 gross.**

**That is one cohort.** The number required to stop a Tokyo municipality shrinking for the first time since the 1960s is smaller than the resident count of a single co-living building.

### It gets easier if it works

The 124/year natural decrease is a function of a 46.9% elderly population. Working-age arrivals with children lower the death share and add births, so **the standstill number falls as you succeed.** This is a real compounding dynamic, not optimism — though it takes years to show, and the first cohort gets no help from it.

## Level 2 — the school threshold, and the finding that changes recruitment

Japanese class formation is defined in law, which means the threshold isn't a judgment call.

**National standard:** a 複式学級 — one class covering two year groups — is formed when two consecutive grades total **≤16 children** (**≤8** where 1st grade is involved).

**Tokyo's own standard is more generous**, which matters because Okutama is in Tokyo: combined classes form when two consecutive grades total ≤10, **but any single grade (other than 1st and 6th) with 6 or more children forms its own class.** 1st and 6th always stand alone.

> **The operative number is six children per school year.**

Below six, a child shares a classroom with another year group. At six, they get their own class. That is a concrete, legally-defined, family-legible line — and it is exactly what §28 *"eleven children her age"* is gesturing at.

### The finding: cohorts must be age-clustered, not just large

Here is what falls out of the arithmetic, and it's the most useful thing in this document.

Suppose 40 households arrive carrying 30 children aged 0–12. Spread naturally, that's 30 children across 13 year groups — **about 2.3 per year.** Not one single grade crosses six. **You have moved 40 households and changed nothing about the school.**

To put six children into a single year group you need to recruit for it deliberately.

> **§28 peers-for-your-child is not a marketing metric. It is a recruitment constraint.**

Consequences, all concrete:

- The intake instrument must capture **children's ages to the year**, not "has children."
- Cohort assembly must **target age bands** — a cohort of 40 households with children clustered at ages 4–6 is worth vastly more to the town than 40 households with children spread evenly, at identical cost.
- This is a **paired threshold** in §31's sense: households *and* an age distribution, cleared together.
- It gives the family-facing promise real teeth. "Eleven children her age" stops being a nice line and becomes the thing the cohort was assembled to deliver.

*Unknown and needed: Okutama's actual school roll by year group. That single table tells you which grades are already below six and therefore exactly which ages to recruit. **Cheapest high-value data request in this whole document.***

## Level 3 — the ladder of success

Four different things could be called success. They have different numbers and they should not be confused.

| Tier | What it means | Roughly |
|---|---|---|
| **0 · The mechanic works** | One threshold clears. People who said "only if others do" actually arrive together. Town-level effect: negligible. | **15–30 households, once** |
| **1 · The town notices** | One institution exists that couldn't before — a school year group at six, a shopfront reopened, a bus route retained. | **cohort + age clustering** |
| **2 · The town stops shrinking** | Net population change reaches zero. First time in six decades. | **40–50 net/yr, 60–75 gross** |
| **3 · The town grows** | Sustained positive net change. | **>50 net/yr, sustained** |

### The warning

**Tier 2 is the one that makes the news. Tier 1 is the one that proves the product.**

If the pilot's stated success criterion is Tier 2 and it lands at Tier 1, that reads publicly as failure — when in fact the thesis was validated and only the scale was short. Set the pilot's criterion at **Tier 1**, state Tier 2 as the trajectory, and never let the press release invert them.

Tier 0 is worth naming separately because it's the only tier that can be reached **without a town's cooperation at all** — it can be demonstrated in the demand test.

## Level 4 — does the money make sense?

The public money is already appropriated and does not depend on Musubu existing.

| Source | Per household |
|---|---|
| 移住支援金 (national/prefectural/municipal) | ¥1M, +¥1M per child |
| Okutama settlement subsidy | up to ¥1M |
| Okutama akiya renovation subsidy | up to ¥2M |

A cohort of 45 households with children plausibly draws **¥90M–180M** of existing public support. **None of that is Musubu's cost to raise** — it is money the state has already committed to exactly this outcome and is currently spending at a trickle, one household at a time.

**So the honest framing of the business case is not "fund the migration."** It is: *the subsidy exists, it is under-consumed, and the missing ingredient is coordination.* Musubu's marginal cost is the coordination layer, not the incentives. That is a far easier thing to fund, and a far easier thing to defend in a town hall.

And it sharpens §S1: against ¥90–180M of public money flowing into a cohort, a funded return for the whole cohort costs on the order of **¥1–3M**. It is a rounding error on the public spend and the single most conversion-relevant line item in it.

## What to actually commit to

For an Okutama-shaped pilot:

- **Target:** 40–50 households net in year one, recruited **age-clustered** to put ≥6 children into at least two specific school years.
- **Stated success criterion:** Tier 1 — one institution that would not otherwise exist.
- **Stated trajectory:** Tier 2 — net zero population change, named as a two-to-three year goal, not a year-one promise.
- **Attribution:** trivially clean, because the town's current net social movement is zero.
- **Headline if it lands:** *a municipality inside Tokyo that the government projected would disappear, stops shrinking.*

## Open questions

1. **Okutama's school roll by year group.** Determines which ages to recruit. One email.
2. **Actual births per year in Okutama.** Confirms the natural-decrease figure and sizes the youngest cohorts.
3. **Housing throughput.** Can the akiya bank and the ¥0 bank actually release 40–50 units in a year? If not, the binding constraint is supply and the target is capped regardless of demand — the same question Hinohara raises in `musubu_tokyo_corridor_candidates.md`.
4. **Does the town want 40–50 households a year?** A 1% annual population change is modest; the *social* change of 45 new households in a town of 4,409 is not. That conversation should happen before the number is published.
5. **Re-run this arithmetic for Yuzawa and Hinohara.** Different populations, different decline compositions, different thresholds. The method transfers; the numbers won't.
