## From Vision to Keys in Hand

## Executive Summary

Housing is where coordination becomes concrete. Families don't commit to abstractionsâ€”they commit when they can see where they'll live, what it will cost, and who their neighbors will be. This strategy outlines how Musubu coordinates housing supply with family demand while minimizing risk for all parties.

**Core Principle:** Seed the vision with early wins, then let coordinated demand drive development at scale.

**Two Parallel Lanes:**
- **Akiya Renovation:** Primary pathway for Phase 1 families (lower cost, faster, heritage preservation)
- **New Construction:** Expansion pathway when akiya inventory is insufficient or specific needs require it

These lanes operate independentlyâ€”progress in one shouldn't block the other.

## The Sequencing Challenge

### The Chicken-and-Egg Problem

Families want to see housing before committing. Developers want commitments before building. Towns want both before investing in infrastructure.

**The Solution: Seed â†’ Coordinate â†’ Scale**

| Stage | What Happens | Purpose |
|-------|--------------|---------|
| **Seed** | Renovate 3-5 akiya as demonstration homes; recruit "pioneer families" willing to move first | Prove it's real, create visible proof, generate stories and photos |
| **Coordinate** | Launch demand-first coordination for remaining housing; match family preferences with available/planned units | Reduce risk through threshold-based commitment; build momentum |
| **Scale** | Expand housing based on proven demand patterns; consider new construction if akiya insufficient | Grow with confidence based on real data |

### Why Seeding Matters

The platform shows the **map** (vision, roadmap, town profiles). But families need to see the **territory** (actual renovated homes, real families living there, tangible progress).

**Seeding accomplishes:**
- Visual proof that akiya can become beautiful homes
- Stories from pioneer families ("We moved, here's what it's like")
- Before/after photos that inspire confidence
- Working relationships with contractors (proven quality)
- Real cost data (not estimates)

**Seeding costs are investment, not speculation**â€”these homes will be occupied, and the learnings de-risk everything that follows.

## Lane 1: Akiya Renovation Pathway

### Why Akiya First

For most Musubu pilot families, renovated akiya will be the primary housing option:

| Factor | Akiya Advantage |

### The Akiya Coordination Process

**Step 1: Inventory & Assessment**

A Housing Coordinator (paid role, see below) surveys available akiya:

| Data Collected | Why It Matters |
|----------------|----------------|
| Location (GPS, neighborhood) | Clustering decisions, walkability to amenities |
| Size (bedrooms, sq meters) | Family matching |
| Condition (structural, systems) | Renovation cost estimation |
| Price (asking, negotiable) | Budget matching |
| Ownership situation | Speed of acquisition |
| Unique features | Appeal to specific families |

**Output:** Akiya database with photos, condition ratings, estimated total cost (purchase + renovation), and location mapping.

**Step 2: Family Preference Collection**

During platform registration, families specify:

| Preference | Options | Coordination Value |
|------------|---------|-------------------|
| Home size | 1-5 bedrooms | Match with appropriate akiya |
| Budget range | Â¥5-10M, Â¥10-15M, Â¥15-20M, etc. | Filter viable options |
| Style preference | Traditional, modern hybrid, fully modernized | Guide renovation approach |
| Location priority | Near school, walkable center, quiet/private, farm-adjacent | Clustering decisions |
| Timeline | Move-ready, 3-6 months, 6-12 months | Renovation scheduling |
| Accessibility needs | Single-story, wheelchair access, etc. | Identify suitable properties |

**Step 3: Matching & Commitment**

| Phase | What Happens |
|-------|--------------|
| **Browse** | Families view available akiya that match their preferences (photos, location, estimated cost) |
| **Express Interest** | Non-binding interest signals help prioritize which akiya to prepare first |
| **Conditional Commit** | Family commits to specific property contingent on renovation meeting quality standards and cost ceiling |
| **Deposit** | Â¥300-500K refundable deposit held in escrow |
| **Renovation** | Work begins; family receives milestone updates |
| **Final Inspection** | Third-party inspection before final payment release |
| **Move-In** | Keys handed over; family becomes resident |

**Step 4: Coordinated Renovation**

Bundling renovations creates efficiency:

| Benefit | How It Works |
|---------|--------------|
| **Contractor Discount** | 10-15% reduction for 5+ simultaneous projects |
| **Bulk Materials** | Shared purchasing of common materials (insulation, fixtures) |
| **Quality Consistency** | Same crews = same standards across homes |
| **Timeline Coordination** | Families move in together (community from day one) |
| **Shared Learning** | Problems solved once, applied everywhere |

### Financial Protection for Akiya

| Protection | Mechanism |
|------------|-----------|
| **Renovation Escrow** | Family deposits released to contractor in 3 milestones (30% start, 40% midpoint, 30% completion) |
| **Cost Ceiling** | Pre-agreed maximum; overruns require explicit approval or covered by contingency fund |
| **Quality Guarantee** | Independent inspection before final payment; contractor warranty period |
| **Government Subsidy Integration** | Â¥1.5M subsidy applied to reduce family cost |
| **Refund Conditions** | Clear terms for deposit return if project fails or family circumstances change |

## Lane 2: New Construction Pathway

### When New Construction Makes Sense

| Situation | Why New Build |
|-----------|---------------|
| Akiya inventory depleted | Demand exceeds available empty houses |
| Specific needs unmet | Accessibility requirements, specific size/layout |
| Phase 2+ expansion | Successful pilot attracts more families than akiya can absorb |
| Clustering strategy | Want to create new neighborhood with consistent design |
| Town preference | Some towns may prefer new development zones |

### Demand-First Coordination for New Builds

Unlike akiya (existing inventory), new construction works best with threshold-based commitment:

**How It Works:**

1. **Design Options Presented:** 3-4 standardized home designs with minor customization (layout variants, fixture choices)

2. **Preference Collection:** Families indicate which design, size, and price point they'd commit to

3. **Threshold Setting:** "Construction begins when 15 families commit to 3-bedroom homes" (threshold based on developer viability)

4. **Commitment Tracking:** Real-time display: "12/15 families committedâ€”3 more needed"

5. **Threshold Reached:** Construction begins; deposits become non-refundable (with reasonable exit conditions)

6. **Phased Construction:** Build in waves as commitment levels allow; adjust future phases based on learnings

### Platform Features for New Construction

| Feature | Function |
|---------|----------|
| **Demand Dashboard** | "47 families want 3-bedroom homes under Â¥15M" |
| **Threshold Tracker** | Progress bars showing commitment levels by home type |
| **Design Gallery** | Visual presentation of available designs with pricing |
| **Commitment Timeline** | "Commit by [date] to be in Phase 1 construction" |
| **Construction Updates** | Milestone photos, completion percentage, projected move-in |

### Developer Coordination

For new construction, developer relationships are essential. Approach depends heavily on **city count strategy** (see below).

| Scale | Developer Approach |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Single city focus** | Partner with 1-2 local/regional developers; deep relationship |
| **Multiple cities simultaneously** | May need national developer partner or developer network; standardized designs across locations |
| **Phased multi-city** | Start local, expand partnerships as model proves out |

## The City Count Strategy Variable

**This is a foundational decision that affects everythingâ€”including housing coordination.**

### Option A: Sequential Focus (1 â†’ 1 â†’ 2 â†’ 1 â†’ 2...)

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Maximum learning from each launch | Slower overall scale |
| Deep relationships with each town | All risk concentrated in one place at a time |
| Full team attention on one location | Limited proof of replicability early |
| Easier to course-correct | May lose momentum between launches |

**Housing Implications:**
- Can build very deep local contractor relationships
- Housing Coordinator can be a single person managing one town
- Developer matching is simpler (local/regional focus)
- Akiya inventory in one town may be sufficient for multiple years

### Option B: Small Parallel Batches (3 â†’ 3 â†’ 5...)

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Tests replicability early | Divided team attention |
| Diversified risk across locations | More complex coordination |
| Faster overall scale | May make same mistakes in multiple places |
| More data for learning | Requires more capital upfront |

**Housing Implications:**
- Need Housing Coordinators in multiple locations (or travel-heavy role)
- May benefit from regional contractor networks
- Different towns may have different akiya conditionsâ€”learnings transfer partially
- New construction threshold may be met faster (3 towns of 50 families = 150 families aggregate demand)

### Option C: Larger Initial Launch (7 â†’ 3 â†’ 3...)

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Bold statement; attracts attention | High risk if model flaws exist |
| Economies of scale from start | Very complex to coordinate |
| Tests model diversity immediately | Requires significant capital and team |
| National presence early | May dilute focus and quality |

**Housing Implications:**
- Likely requires national developer partnership or standardized design packages
- Housing coordination platform becomes essential (can't manage 7 towns manually)
- Different regions have very different housing markets
- Some towns may succeed while others struggleâ€”risk of visible failure alongside success

### Recommendation: Start Sequential, Prepare for Parallel

**Phase 1:** Single city (Bungotakada) with full focus
**Phase 2:** If successful, launch 2 cities (including a different town type like Namie)
**Phase 3:** If both succeed, consider 3-5 simultaneous, now with proven playbook

This allows deep learning while building toward parallel capacity.

**Housing coordination should be designed to scale**â€”even if starting with one town, build the platform features and processes that will work across multiple towns later.

## Housing by Town Type

Different town types have different housing needs. The coordination strategy should adapt:

| Town Type | Primary Housing Need | Coordination Emphasis |
|-----------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **Family Growth Town** | 3-4 bedroom family homes; multi-generational options; proximity to childcare/schools | Cluster families with young children; prioritize akiya near schools |
| **Tech Training Village** | Mix of family homes + smaller units for young professionals/couples; fiber internet essential | Ensure all units have high-speed connectivity; co-working proximity |
| **Creative Hub** | Live-work spaces; studio integration; adaptable structures | Identify akiya with outbuildings or large rooms for studios |
| **Agricultural** | Farmhouses with land; equipment storage; proximity to fields | Different inventoryâ€”rural akiya with land, not town-center properties |
| **Heritage Preserve** | Traditional architecture preserved; craft workshop space | Renovation approach emphasizes preservation over modernization |

**Platform Implementation:** When families select town type interest, housing preference options should reflect that type's needs.

## Clustering vs. Spreading: A Town-Specific Decision

### Clustering (Dense Musubu Neighborhoods)

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|------------|---------------|
| Immediate neighbor community | May feel isolated from broader town |
| Co-op coordination easier | Could create "us vs. them" dynamic |
| Shared infrastructure efficient | Less integration with existing residents |
| Visual impact ("Musubu district") | Limits housing choice |

### Spreading (Distributed Throughout Town)

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|------------|---------------|
| Integration with existing community | Harder to build neighbor bonds initially |
| More housing options available | Co-op logistics more complex |
| Avoids enclave perception | Less visible impact |
| Diverse neighborhood experiences | May feel isolated if too spread |

### Recommendation: Strategic Clustering

**Default:** Identify 2-3 "Musubu clusters" within the townâ€”neighborhoods with multiple available akiya where 5-10 families can be neighbors. This provides:
- Immediate community (walking distance to several other Musubu families)
- Integration (clusters are within existing neighborhoods, not separate)
- Flexibility (families can choose which cluster suits their preferences)
- Visibility (clusters show tangible change)

**Exception:** Some families may prefer more isolated/private locationsâ€”accommodate if inventory allows, but explain trade-offs.

## Co-Living Option (For Tech Villages / Young Professionals)

For certain demographics, shared housing arrangements may be more appropriate than individual family homes.

### Model: Shared Houses (Oakhouse-style)

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Structure** | Private bedrooms, shared living/kitchen/bath |
| **Target** | Young professionals, tech trainees, singles, couples without children |
| **Cost** | Â¥30-50K/month per person (much lower than individual unit) |
| **Social** | Built-in community; natural interaction |
| **Management** | Requires house manager or rotating responsibilities |

### When Appropriate

- Tech Training Village with significant young professional population
- Transitional housing (families testing before committing to purchase)
- Budget-conscious individuals
- Short-term residents (contract workers, seasonal)

### When NOT Appropriate

- Families with children (need privacy, stability)
- Long-term permanent residents
- People who prioritize space/quiet
- Most Family Growth Town residents

### Integration with Musubu

- **Position as option**, not default
- Offer alongside akiya and new construction for towns with relevant demographics
- Could renovate large akiya into shared houses (multiple bedrooms, expanded common areas)
- May serve as "entry point" before families transition to individual homes

## Risk Mitigation Summary

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| **Family commits, then circumstances change** | Clear refund terms with reasonable exit windows; deposits partially refundable until renovation begins |
| **Renovation costs exceed estimates** | Cost ceilings with contingency fund; milestone-based payments; multiple contractor bids |
| **Contractor quality issues** | Vetted contractor network; independent inspections; warranty requirements |
| **Akiya has hidden problems** | Professional inspection before family commitment; seller disclosure requirements |
| **Not enough families commit** | Seed phase proves model; lower thresholds for initial phases; marketing to build pipeline |
| **Developer backs out** | Contracts with penalties; escrow protection; backup developer relationships |
| **Government subsidy changes** | Conservative budgeting that doesn't depend entirely on subsidies; diversified funding |

## Conclusion

Housing coordination is where Musubu's promise becomes tangible. The strategy must balance:

- **Speed** (families want to move) with **Quality** (homes must be good)
- **Certainty** (families need to know what they're getting) with **Flexibility** (circumstances change)
- **Efficiency** (coordinate for cost savings) with **Choice** (families have different needs)
- **Vision** (show the plan) with **Proof** (show it working)

By running akiya renovation and new construction as parallel lanes, seeding with early wins before full coordination, and building platform features that scale across towns, Musubu can solve the housing coordination challenge while minimizing risk for families, towns, and the project itself.

**The goal:** Every family that commits to Musubu has a clear path to a home they'll love, at a cost they can afford, ready when they need it, surrounded by neighbors who chose to be there too.
