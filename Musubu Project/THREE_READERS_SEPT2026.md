# Three Readers
## What a municipal officer, a Fukuoka founder and a 23-year-old in Tokyo would each say first, and what the corpus does and does not answer

*Written 2026-09-17 by Claude (Fable 5.1) after reading the full corpus, as the second of three tasks Wai set for the September consolidation. The August Gemini audit read stale files and restated the retired framing; this one reads the current corpus and takes three specific seats. The readers are composites, not real people, and the objections are the ones the documents themselves make possible. Where the corpus has an answer the file is named. Where it does not, that is the finding.*

---

## How to use this

Each reader gets the same five things: what they would say in the first thirty seconds, their strongest objection, what in the corpus answers it, what does not, and the one sentence most likely to land with them. The synthesis at the end lists what recurs across all three, because an objection three different people make from three different seats is structural rather than a matter of taste.

Nothing here is a reason to change the register. `musubu_voice_and_posture.md` holds. The point is to know where the pages are exposed before a stranger finds out for you.

## Reader 1 · A planning officer in a town of 20,000
*企画課, mid-forties, has processed 移住支援金 applications and hosted two 協力隊 members, one of whom left early. Reads the concept page on a phone because a colleague forwarded it.*

### First thirty seconds

*"Who is this, and who is behind them?"* Then, on seeing the answer: *"So nobody."* The page says it is a concept and not a company in the first line, which is honest, and honesty here costs attention. A town officer's day is made of things that come with an institution attached. A thing that does not will be read as a hobby unless something on the page changes that in the first screen.

### The strongest objection

*"We already do this."* The town has an 空き家バンク, applies the national relocation grant, has hosted 協力隊 members, runs お試し住宅 trial stays, and has a relocation page with photographs of rice terraces. From inside the town office, Musubu looks like a fifth relocation programme with a progress bar.

### What answers it

`musubu_precedent_research.md` §3 is the strongest material in the corpus for this reader and it is not on the concept page in its sharpest form. *Japan already has a programme that retains eight in ten of the people it places, and half of them arrive to find it was not what they were told. We are not inventing placement. We are fixing the matching.* An officer who has watched a 協力隊 member leave early knows exactly what mismatch means. The 80/50 pair is on the concept page as a lesson learned; it should be the first thing this reader sees.

`musubu_precedent_research.md` §5 has the second sentence: Japan has moved three hundred households together, with a legal framework and a subsidy, every time a volcano erupted. It has never done it voluntarily. An officer knows 防災集団移転. Nobody has ever put that programme next to relocation policy for them.

### What does not answer it

**The existing residents have no voice anywhere in the artifacts.** This is the largest gap the corpus has for this reader and it is not on `OPEN_THREADS.md` in these terms. A planning officer's real fear is not that newcomers fail to come. It is that thirty households arrive, do not join the 自治会, get the rubbish days wrong, complain about the 消防団 and leave after two years, and the officer is the one who has to face the residents' association afterwards. `musubu_portal_taxonomy.md` gives residents a *view*. `musubu_2032_vision.md` renders the existing town in quiet slate so the map does not read as a takeover. Nothing gives the people already there a way to say what they will and will not accept, and nothing shows them what a newcomer has agreed to before arriving. The party with the veto has no portal.

**There is no month one.** `musubu_voice_and_posture.md` says so itself: every prototype is a vision artifact and no day-one set exists. The first question this officer asks is what actually happens in the first ninety days, and the answer is fifteen pages showing year five.

**The deposit is a regulated activity nobody has examined.** The officer will not know which law, but will know it is somebody's law. `OPEN_THREADS.md` #12 and #54 mark this as needing a lawyer. It stays a dealbreaker until it has one.

### The sentence that lands

> *Your town already knows how to receive people who were placed there by a programme. Half of them arrived to find it was not what they were told. This is a way to fix the telling.*

## Reader 2 · A founder in Fukuoka
*Second company, one exit, bilingual, has sat through a hundred pitches at Fukuoka Growth Next. Reads the coordination-layer page first because a friend said it was the technical one.*

### First thirty seconds

*"Nice screen. Where's the wedge?"* This reader is not asking whether the idea is good. They are asking what the first thing is that can exist without a town, a law firm or a ministry, and whether it earns anything.

### The strongest objection

*"Two-sided marketplace, no side."* There are no towns and no pledgers. Every marketplace pitch dies here, and this one has the added problem that the supply side is a municipality with a four-year election cycle and the demand side is asking people to bind a life decision on the terms they would bind ¥5,000.

### What answers it

`musubu_coordination_architecture.md` §6, rule 2: run the demand board before any town is signed. The board needs no permission and demand is the asset that makes every later conversation easy. `musubu_coordination_theory.md` §3 adds the piece the founder will want: a refund bonus makes the first pledge rational before anyone else has pledged, which is a real answer to cold start rather than a hope. §2's self-set thresholds turn the board from a counter into a measurement, which is the kind of thing a founder can see a product in.

On the "life decision on Kickstarter terms" point, `musubu_demand_test.md` has the right posture: that transfer risk is exactly what the test measures, and both outcomes are worth more than another document. A founder respects a pre-registered readout.

### What does not answer it

**The revenue model depends on municipalities, and the corpus knows it.** `musubu_fukuoka_pitch.md` lists coordination contracts, licences and retention-linked fees, all with a town or prefecture as the buyer. A founder in Japan knows what selling to municipalities means: procurement, fiscal years, a champion who gets rotated in April. The "honest de-risking note" about grant funding is honest, and grant-funded is what a founder hears when someone says "not venture-scale."

**There is no moat, and the project does not want one.** `musubu_precedent_research.md` §6 says the competitors list houses and nobody assembles people. The founder's reply: *then ADDress adds a counter next quarter.* The corpus's real answer is that it is a contribution, not a company, and Wai would rather it existed than owned it. That is disarming in the room and it is also the sentence that tells an investor there is nothing to invest in. `OPEN_THREADS.md` #39 asks whether "I don't want to own it" reads as clarity or low conviction. For this reader it reads as: *this is a public good, and public goods are funded differently.* Which is true, and worth saying that way.

**140,000 words and zero respondents is a smell.** The founder will say it, kindly. The corpus's own line (`musubu_demand_test.md`: "a closed loop that produces internal consistency easily mistaken for truth") is the right response and should be nearer the front.

**No Japanese co-founder.** `OPEN_THREADS.md` #40. The founder will ask in the first five minutes and there is no answer except *that is part of the ask*.

### The sentence that lands

> *The share house is the mirror at its smallest. The product is the mirror: a count of what people privately want that nobody had to be first to say. Nothing else in Japan holds that number.*

## Reader 3 · Twenty-three, Tokyo, second year in a job she does not like
*Lives in a share house in Nakano. Found the page because someone posted it. Reads `musubu-twenties.html` because the title spoke to her, then the concept page.*

### First thirty seconds

*"Is this a cult, a scam, or a 移住 campaign?"* Every young person in Japan has seen rural relocation marketing and a fair number have seen something that started warm and turned out to be selling something. The concept page's plainness helps enormously here. The word "cohort" does not. Neither does "coordination layer." The twenties page is right to abandon the warm serif and state numbers.

### The strongest objection

*"Everyone will think I failed."* `musubu_life_stage_doors.md` names this precisely: a 23-year-old moving rurally risks being read as having given up, and no economic argument touches it. The corpus is honest that it has no answer except naming it.

### What answers it

The twenties page's money table (¥21,000 left a month in Tokyo against ¥103,000 in a share house in a town of five thousand) is the strongest single artifact for this reader, because it does not ask her to want the countryside. It asks her to do arithmetic. The "what is worse" list at the same size as "what is better" earns trust with someone who is suspicious of being marketed to.

`musubu_coordination_theory.md` §1 gives her something the older documents do not: **出る杭は打たれる, and this is a way of not being the stake.** She is not the first to go. Nobody is. That is a status argument, not an economic one, and it is the only kind that reaches the objection.

§4's paired pledge is the other one. *I go if Yuki goes.* She would not consider this alone and she has one friend who might. The corpus talked about recruiting groups from the supply side; from her seat, the unit is a friend.

The funded return (`musubu_funded_return_model.md`) and the density ladder answer *can I get out*, which is her second question.

### What does not answer it

**Everything on the screen is fake, and she notices.** The illustrative labels that reassure a ministry alienate her. "Kamisato is not a real town and none of these numbers are real" is the honest footer and it is also the moment she closes the tab, because what she wanted was to see who else was going and there is nobody. This is not fixable with copy. It is fixable only by the demand test running and a real count appearing, which is the corpus's own first step. Until then the twenties page is a promise, and she has read promises.

**"Learn carpentry" is one person's dream.** The illustrative examples lean on trades, kindergartens and cafés. She works in digital marketing and does not want to rethatch a roof. `musubu_archetype_axes.md` has the breadth; the public pages show one archetype.

**She would do this in Fukuoka city, not in a town.** `musubu_coordination_architecture.md` §2, district hosting, is written for exactly her, and it is the least visible idea in the corpus. For this reader it is the most attractive one.

### The sentence that lands

> *You are twenty-three. There is a third option, and you would not be the first one there.*

## Synthesis · what all three say

Five things recur, from three unrelated seats. These are structural.

1. **The existing residents are absent.** The officer fears them, the founder knows they are the real supply-side risk, and the 23-year-old wonders whether the town actually wants her. Nothing in fifteen prototypes is addressed to the people who already live there. **Add to `OPEN_THREADS.md` as a new thread, tagged `BUILD`: a resident-facing surface, showing what a newcomer has agreed to and letting the town say what it will and will not accept.**

2. **Year five is drawn; month one is not.** Named in `musubu_voice_and_posture.md`, still true. The founder and the officer both ask the same question in different words.

3. **Illustrative reassures institutions and alienates the young.** The labels are correct and non-negotiable. The tension resolves only when a real number exists, which is the argument for the demand test being first, made a fourth time from a fourth direction.

4. **Legal and money are dealbreakers in waiting.** The deposit's regulatory status and the municipal revenue dependence. Both known, both parked, both the first thing a serious reader raises.

5. **The vocabulary is split.** The concept page speaks plainly. The technical page and most of the corpus say cohort, threshold, coordination layer, instrument, mechanic. Those are the right words inside the project and the wrong words in front of a Japanese reader under thirty. A glossary is not the fix. Choosing which page each reader lands on is, which is what `Portal/index.html` now does.

### What the corpus already has and should move forward

- The 80/50 pair, to the front of anything a municipality sees.
- The volcano sentence, next to it.
- District hosting, out of §2 of an architecture document and onto a page a 23-year-old would find.
- The leverage-point answer to "why would yours work" (`musubu_coordination_theory.md` §6), into the pitch.

### What it does not have

- A resident-facing artifact.
- A day-one artifact.
- A lawyer's paragraph on the deposit.
- A single respondent.

The last is the one the other three wait on, and it is the one the project's author has said plainly he is not the person to get.
