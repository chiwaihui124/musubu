## The tone rule

Agreed and worth writing down, because it's also strategically correct:

> **Don't arrive with an answer. Arrive with a specific question and a genuine gap.**

Municipal officers, migration-desk staff and 協力隊 alumni are all pitched at constantly. A precise question is rare, cheap to answer, and starts a relationship that a proposal would foreclose. It also has the advantage of being true — the project genuinely doesn't know these things yet.

Corollary: **do not imply a programme is coming.** Nothing has been promised to anyone, and hinting otherwise creates an obligation that can't yet be met.

## 1 · Okutama — the school roll

**Send first. Smallest possible move, cannot fail badly.**

**Check before sending:** the 教育委員会 may already publish 学校要覧 with 児童数 by year. If so, don't spend goodwill asking for public data — read it, and send a better question instead (see variant below).

**Route to:** the 移住・定住 desk rather than 教育委員会. They'll forward it if needed, and it starts the relationship where you actually want it.

### Japanese

> **件名：小学校の学年別児童数について（お問い合わせ）**
>
> 奥多摩町役場　移住・定住ご担当者様
>
> 突然のご連絡を差し上げます失礼をお許しください。
> 〇〇と申します。地方への移住を「一世帯ずつ」ではなく「複数世帯がまとまって」進める仕組みについて individually 調べており、その参考として奥多摩町の取り組みを拝見しております。
>
> 一点だけお伺いできればと存じます。
> 町内の小学校について、**学年別の児童数**が分かる公表資料はございますでしょうか。公表されたものがない場合は、差し支えのない範囲で概数をご教示いただけますと幸いです。
>
> お伺いする理由ですが、子育て世帯が移住を検討する際、「同じ学年に何人いるか」が判断を大きく左右すると考えており、そこを正確に理解したいためです。
>
> なお、現段階で何かご提案を持ち込む趣旨ではございません。まずは実情を正しく把握したいという段階です。
>
> お忙しいところ恐れ入りますが、ご教示いただけますと幸いです。
>
> 〇〇
> （連絡先）

*Note: remove the stray English word in line 2 before sending — placeholder from drafting.*

### English (for your own reference)

> **Subject: Enquiry — primary school pupil numbers by year group**
>
> To the relocation and settlement desk, Okutama Town Office
>
> Apologies for the unsolicited contact. My name is —. I'm researching how relocation to rural areas might work when households move *together* rather than one at a time, and I've been looking at Okutama's approach as part of that.
>
> One question, if I may: is there any published material showing **pupil numbers by year group** for the town's primary schools? If nothing is published, an approximate figure would be very helpful.
>
> The reason I ask: for families considering a move, *how many children are in the same school year* seems to weigh heavily on the decision, and I'd like to understand that accurately.
>
> I should say I'm not at the stage of bringing any proposal — I'm trying to understand the actual situation first.
>
> Thank you for your time.

### Variant, if the data is already public

Then ask the question the data raises instead:
> 公表資料を拝見しました。〇年生が〇名と、複式学級の基準に近い学年があるように見受けられます。今後の見通しについて、町として何か想定されている対応はございますでしょうか。

*(I've seen the published figures. Year X appears to be near the combined-class threshold. Is there a response the town is anticipating?)*

That question demonstrates you've done the work and asks something only they can answer — a much stronger opening than a data request.

## 2 · ふるさと回帰支援センター — the highest-information hour available

The national relocation consultation centre, based in Yūrakuchō, Tokyo. Their advisers speak to **hundreds of prospective migrants a year** and hear the blockers first-hand, repeatedly, across every prefecture. Nobody in this project has that pattern knowledge, and one conversation would transfer a lot of it.

They run free consultations and also online sessions.

**Be honest about who you are.** You're not personally relocating — you're researching the coordination problem. Advisers will still talk; researchers are interesting to them. Pretending otherwise would be both dishonest and quickly obvious.

### Japanese

> **件名：移住相談の現場についてお話を伺えないでしょうか**
>
> ふるさと回帰支援センター　ご担当者様
>
> 突然のご連絡失礼いたします。〇〇と申します。
>
> 私は現在、地方移住において「複数の世帯が同時期にまとまって移る」場合に何が起きるのか、という仕組みについて調べております。移住を個人的に検討している立場ではなく、恐縮ながら調査・研究の趣旨でのご連絡です。
>
> 相談窓口で日々多くの方と接しておられる皆様に、以下のような点を伺えればと考えております。
>
> ・相談に来られた方が、最終的に移住に至らない場合、その理由として最も多いものは何でしょうか
> ・「一人だけ／一世帯だけ先に移る」ことへの不安は、実際に語られることがありますか
> ・逆に、移住に踏み切れた方に共通する条件のようなものはございますか
>
> 短いお時間でも、またオンラインでも構いません。ご相談窓口の趣旨と異なるお願いであることは承知しておりますので、難しい場合は遠慮なくお申し付けください。
>
> 〇〇
> （連絡先）

### The three questions, in English

1. When someone comes for a consultation and *doesn't* end up moving, what's the most common reason?
2. Does the fear of being the only one — moving alone, ahead of anyone else — actually come up in conversation, or is that not how people describe it?
3. Conversely, is there anything common to the people who do go through with it?

**Question 2 is the one that matters.** It tests the project's founding assumption directly, from people with the largest sample in Japan. If the answer is *"no, that's not really what they say"* — that is enormously valuable to learn now, for free, before anything is built.

## 3 · The people who left — the interviews nobody does

Roughly **31% of 地域おこし協力隊 members leave the region** after their term (総務省, 8,034 people, FY2019–23). That's about 2,500 people who tried rural life with support and structure, and still went. They know exactly what breaks.

**No directory exists.** The realistic route is people who've written publicly:

- search note.com and personal blogs for 「地域おこし協力隊 辞めた」「任期終了 都会に戻った」「協力隊 失敗」
- 協力隊 OB/OG networks, and JOIN (移住・交流推進機構)
- people who wrote a 退任 post and then stopped posting — often the most candid, and never asked

### Japanese

> **件名：協力隊のご経験について、少しお話を伺えませんか**
>
> 〇〇様
>
> はじめまして。〇〇と申します。
> ノートに書かれていた〇〇についての記事を拝読しました。任期を終えられたあとのお話を、正直に書かれている方はあまり多くないと感じ、ご連絡いたしました。
>
> 私はいま、地方移住について「一人ずつ移る」のではなく「複数世帯が同時に移る」場合に何が変わるのか、という仕組みを調べています。その中で、**実際に地域を離れた方のお話こそ一番参考になる**と考えております。制度の紹介記事は数多くありますが、離れた理由を語られたものはほとんど見当たりません。
>
> もしご迷惑でなければ、30分ほどお話を伺えないでしょうか。伺いたいのは主に次の点です。
>
> ・離れると決めた時期と、そのきっかけ
> ・もし何かひとつ違っていれば残っていた、と思われることはありますか
> ・帰るための費用や手続きで、負担に感じられたことはありましたか
>
> ご記事を批判する意図はまったくございません。また、お名前を出す予定もありません。ご都合が合わなければ、どうぞお気になさらないでください。
>
> 〇〇

### The three questions, in English

1. When did you decide to leave, and what triggered it?
2. Is there one thing that, had it been different, would have kept you there?
3. Was the cost or logistics of going back a burden?

**Question 3 is the funded return's validation.** If leavers say the trip home was a real burden, §S1 has direct evidence. If they say it was trivial and the reasons were entirely social or economic, that's a significant correction — and one worth having before spending anything on it.

## Order and reasoning

1. **Okutama email.** Today. One question, no attachment, no proposal. Converts the project from concept to contact at the lowest possible stakes.
2. **ふるさと回帰支援センター.** This week. Tests the founding assumption with the largest sample in Japan, for free.
3. **Two or three leavers.** Over a few weeks. Slowest to arrange, highest signal, and validates §S1 specifically.

**Fukuoka is not on this list deliberately.** Startup ecosystem people assess business models and fundability — neither is the live question. Come back to them when there's something to fund.
