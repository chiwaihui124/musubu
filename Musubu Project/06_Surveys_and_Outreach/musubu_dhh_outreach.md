# Note to David Heinemeier Hansson

*Written 2026-08-30. Third pass, after Wai corrected both the framing and the posture.*
*Companion to `Portal/musubu-coordination-layer.html`.*

A friendly note to DHH after his Lex Fridman episode. **Not an ask, not a pitch,
and not a cold email.** Wai's own description of the goal:

> *"I was listening to the podcast. I thought this was an interesting thing that
> I've been working on and I want to share and get some friendly feedback. I find
> some of the things that you were saying enlightening and inspiring. That is the
> goal."*

That is the whole brief. Nothing in this file should read as strategy, because
there is no strategy. Somebody heard something good and wants to show a person
their work.

---

## Two earlier passes, and why they were wrong

Kept because the corrections are worth not repeating.

**Pass one** was built on DHH's public reputation as a sceptic of platforms and
agent infrastructure, so it was written to survive a hostile reader and closed by
attacking its own premise. Wai: *"the threads that you decided to pull on were
different threads than I might be pulling on."* What he actually took from the
episode was flow, joy and wanting more people building things.

**Pass two** fixed the framing but kept the posture: subject-line optimisation, a
single engineered question, an argument about how to survive the first three
lines. Wai: *"it's not an ask, so you don't have to worry about it being a cold
email and worrying about how it sounds. We can just be honest and authentic."*

**Now:** say the true thing plainly, share the link, ask for a friendly reaction.

## Things Wai wants input on

On the page these are the closing section, which is what turns it from a
presentation into something a reader can join. The note asks only the first and
points at the rest, because five questions in a message body is a homework
assignment.

1. **Is there a seed of something here?**
2. **Is the national version realistic?** A shared ledger of knowledge any
   citizen can write into, legible enough that policy can be written against it.
3. **Is building for change a strength or an excuse?** It is meant to keep
   growing new tools and interfaces as people need them.
4. **Would it change anything, and is it worth pursuing?**
5. **What would you improve, and which thread would you pull first?**
6. **What blockers do you see that are not already listed?**
