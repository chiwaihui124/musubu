**Part 1: Strategic Role of the Survey**

The Musubu platform faces a bootstrapping problem common to all
coordination platforms: it only becomes valuable when it has
participants, but it is hard to attract participants before it has
momentum to show. The national survey solves this problem by generating
the first wave of visible demand before the platform is fully built.

But the survey does more than gather data. It is the first act of the
movement.

**1.1 What the Survey Actually Does**

|                                 |                                    |
| -   A research tool             | -   The first act of coordination  |

|                                 |     community                      |
+---------------------------------+------------------------------------+

**1.2 Five Jobs the Survey Performs**

1.  Validates individual experience. Each question says: your struggle
    is real, your desires are legitimate, you are not asking for too
    much. This is psychologically powerful for people who have been
    quietly frustrated but assumed they were the only ones.

2.  Aggregates latent demand into visible proof. Scattered individuals
    become a data point: 2,145 families seriously considering this. That
    number is more persuasive to governments, companies, and future
    participants than any business plan.

3.  Builds the first community asset: the email list. Everyone who opts
    in becomes reachable. This is the platform's user base before the
    platform exists.

4.  Generates media fuel. Survey results with human scale ("78% of Tokyo
    families say they cannot afford children") are exactly the kind of
    data Japanese media covers. Every story drives more survey
    responses. Virtuous cycle.

5.  Provides platform intelligence. Town type preferences, barrier
    rankings, incentive sensitivity, and geographic distribution all
    directly inform product and pilot town decisions.

**Part 2: The Psychological Architecture**

The questions are not neutral. They are designed to move the respondent
through a specific psychological journey: from named frustration, to
articulated aspiration, to recognised barriers, to imagined possibility,
to commitment.

**2.1 Questions as Generative, Not Extractive**

+---------------------------------+------------------------------------+
| **Extractive question (bad)**   | **Generative question (good)**     |
|                                 |                                    |
| "Where do you live?"            | "What are your biggest challenges  |
|                                 | right now?"                        |
| "Are you happy?"                |                                    |
|                                 | "If you could design your ideal    |
| "Would you move to a rural      | life 5 years from now, what would  |
| area?"                          | it include?"                       |
|                                 |                                    |
|                                 | "What would you need to feel safe  |
|                                 | making a major change?"            |
+---------------------------------+------------------------------------+

The difference: generative questions make people think about what is
wrong, articulate what they want, and realise that the barriers they
face are removable. By the end of the survey, the respondent has done
intellectual and emotional work that prepares them to believe in Musubu.

**2.2 The Six-Part Psychological Journey**

6.  **Part 1: Where You Are Now ---** Name the pain

7.  **Part 2: What You Want ---** Articulate the dream

8.  **Part 3: What's Stopping You ---** Surface the barriers

9.  **Part 4: The Possibility ---** Introduce the solution

10. **Part 5: Making It Real ---** Invite participation

11. **Part 6: Demographics ---** Understand the audience

**Part 7: What the Data Unlocks**

The survey's value extends far beyond the individual respondent
relationship. The aggregate data becomes Musubu's most powerful asset
for every category of stakeholder.

**7.1 For Families Considering Participation**

Proof they are not alone. Seeing that 2,000+ others share the same fears
and desires removes the social stigma of 'I must be the only one
thinking this way.' This is the primary emotional barrier the data
dissolves.

**7.2 For Government Stakeholders**

Survey data showing 30--40% of urban respondents seriously considering
rural migration is compelling evidence for budget allocation. It
transforms Musubu from a startup pitch into a policy-relevant demand
signal. It gives sympathetic ministry officials the internal
justification to support the programme.

**7.3 For Rural Towns**

Towns can see real demand data filtered by town type preference. A town
focused on eldercare can see exactly how many respondents listed 'Family
Growth Town' as their first choice, and where they are currently
located. This turns abstract potential into concrete pipeline.

**7.4 For Company Partners**

The survey's Q11 (work preferences) and Q5 (current industry) data,
combined with Q19 (town type interest), creates a talent profile for
each community. A company considering rural hiring can see the skills,
experience, and salary expectations of people who are already
considering moving. This is more persuasive than any business
development pitch.

**7.5 For Investors and Accelerators**

Survey data is the closest thing to validated demand a pre-product
platform can show. It replaces speculative market sizing with real human
responses. A Fukuoka investor can see that X% of respondents are already
in Kyushu and that Y% cite Bungotakada-adjacent preferences. This is the
proof that unlocks the seed round.

**7.6 The Living Dashboard**

It is not a research exercise. It is where the movement begins.
