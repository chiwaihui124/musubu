# Musubu — Town Intelligence & Community Vibe System
## Reference Document

*Concepts developed from Oak House co-living model insight, April 2026*

---

## The Core Insight: Oak House as Proof of Concept

Oak House (Japanese co-living company) displays resident composition charts on each property listing — age breakdown, nationality mix, visa type. This lets prospective residents self-select based on community vibe before ever visiting.

**The key observation:** People aren't choosing a unit. They're choosing a social environment.

The charts do two things simultaneously:
- Describe who's already there
- Help the visitor decide if they belong

A 40-year-old professional sees a floor that's 80% student/working holiday visa holders and immediately knows it's not for them — without reading a word. That's efficient, honest communication.

**The Musubu translation:** Town portals should do the same — not for the existing (largely elderly) population, but for the **incoming cohort**. The people who've committed or are seriously interested. That's the community a newcomer would actually be joining.

## The Two-Layer Community Profile System

### Layer 1 — Town's Declared Direction
Set during onboarding, revisitable over time. The town coordinator answers 8–12 questions about vision and values — not dry demographics, but directional choices:

- "We want to be known for..."
- "The kind of person who'd thrive here..."
- "Our priority for the next 5 years is..."

These translate into a soft profile weighted toward a town archetype:
- **Family Growth Town** — schools, childcare, family events
- **Youth Energy Hub** — remote work, co-working, young community
- **Creative Community** — makers, artists, cultural programming
- **Simple Life Town** — quiet, nature, slow living
- **Startup Village** — entrepreneurship, innovation cluster

### Layer 2 — Actual Interest Signal
Emerges from survey respondents as they favorite towns, make commitments, and move through the platform journey. If 40 remote workers in their 30s all favorite Bungotakada, that shapes the live signal.

**The compatibility score** is the alignment (or productive tension) between these two layers — not a single number, but a fingerprint comparison between declared intention and revealed interest.

### When They Diverge
A town that declared "young families" but is attracting solo creatives isn't necessarily wrong — it's a conversation starter. The data advises the town: *"You said families, but creatives keep showing up — do you want to lean into that?"*

## Town Intelligence Dashboard — What Towns Should See

### Tab 1: Demand Overview
National survey aggregate data filtered to what's relevant for this town type.

Key metrics to display:
- % of respondents seriously considering relocation
- % who'd move within 2 years (the platform's action window)
- Demand by town type nationally (so towns see where they sit)
- Desired community vibe breakdown
- Move-readiness timeline histogram

**Purpose:** Towns stop guessing what urban families want. They see it with real numbers.

### Tab 2: Who's Looking — Life Stage Segments
Five segments with click-through detail:

| Segment | ~% of Demand | Key Need | Move Readiness |
|---------|-------------|----------|----------------|
| Families w/ Young Kids | 38% | Schools + other families | 72% within 2 years |
| Young Couples (No Kids) | 25% | Affordable space + community | 51% within 2 years |
| Remote Workers (Solo) | 18% | Broadband + social life | 44% within 1 year |
| Empty Nesters | 11% | Purpose + healthcare | 2–3 year horizon |
| Retirees / Semi-retired | 8% | Healthcare + quiet | 3+ year horizon |

Each segment card shows: priorities, dealbreakers, readiness, and a town-specific fit assessment.

**Purpose:** Town coordinators understand their actual audience, not a demographic abstraction.

### Tab 3: What They Want — Priorities & Dealbreakers
- Radar chart of how strongly each factor influences decisions
- Dealbreaker ranking (what would stop a committed family)
- Town readiness assessment against each factor (strong / moderate / developing)

**Top priorities nationally (all segments combined):**
1. Good schools (88% weight)
2. Community feel (82%)
3. Healthcare access (79%)
4. Nature access (76%)
5. Low cost of living (71%)
6. Job/income options (65%)

**Top dealbreakers:**
1. No healthcare nearby (74%)
2. Spouse has no job path (68%)
3. Poor school quality (63%)
4. No broadband / remote work infrastructure (58%)
5. Too isolated / no community (55%)
6. No childcare options (49%)

### Tab 4: Set Your Direction
Town chooses its archetype based on demand data — informed, not aspirational. Each option shows:
- National demand size for that type
- Town's fit score for that direction
- What infrastructure gaps exist

Direction choice shapes portal matching and can be updated as real interest data evolves.

## The Survey as Intelligence Product

The national survey ("Japan 2040") is not just a coordination tool for families — it is the most valuable market research a rural town has ever had access to.

### What towns normally do
Guess. Make brochures based on what they think sounds appealing. Target broadly. Hope.

### What Musubu gives them
Real aggregate demand data, filterable by segment. A town focused on eldercare can see exactly how many respondents listed Family Growth as first choice, where they're currently located, and what their dealbreakers are.

**This transforms the ask to towns.** Instead of: *"Take a leap of faith and join our platform."*

It becomes: *"Before you commit to anything, we'll show you exactly what demand exists for what you could offer. You're making a data-informed decision."*

## The Living Intelligence Model — Data Layers Over Time

The dashboard evolves as more people move through the Musubu journey. Four distinct data layers emerge:

### Layer 1 — Survey Intent Data (Day 1)
- Stated preferences, dealbreakers, life stage
- Broad but static
- Thousands of data points from launch

### Layer 2 — Platform Behavior (Months 1–6)
- Which towns people favorite
- Time spent on each portal
- Questions asked, return visits
- **Revealed preference is more honest than stated preference**

### Layer 3 — Commitment Signals (Months 6–18)
- Who made conditional commitments
- Which towns are filling fastest
- What triggered the jump from "interested" to "committed"
- Tipping point moments become visible

### Layer 4 — Post-Move Data (Months 18+)
- Satisfaction reports from settled residents
- What surprised them
- What they wish they'd known
- **Critically: what finally made them take the leap**

## The "What Made People Leap" Intelligence

As post-move data accumulates, the platform can surface the actual behavioral triggers that converted interest into action. This feeds back to towns as actionable guidance — not survey speculation, but behavioral evidence.

**Example insight a town might receive:**
*"The top three things that converted interested families into committed ones in towns like yours were: (1) meeting a family who'd already moved, (2) a trial visit weekend, (3) seeing the school in person."*

A town can directly act on this. It becomes a conversion optimization tool, not just a data display.

## The Quarterly Pipeline View

As the platform matures, it gains a predictive layer distinct from intent data:

| Signal | What It Enables |
|--------|----------------|
| "20% of committed families will move next quarter" | Towns prepare housing, schools, welcome events |
| "10% of moved residents have been settled 3+ months" | Real testimonials, proof of life stories |
| "Current most common tipping points" | Towns can engineer those moments |
| "Segment X is moving faster than declared" | Towns can adjust their direction weighting |

**The platform becomes genuinely predictive, not just descriptive.** Government can front-load subsidies. Companies can time hiring. Towns can prepare infrastructure. This is the intelligence layer that justifies Musubu as national coordination infrastructure, not just a migration website.

## The Resident Composition Display (Public-Facing)

On the public town portal (visible to prospective residents), the composition display shows the incoming cohort — not the existing population.

### Useful metrics for display

**Life stage breakdown** — single most decision-relevant signal. Someone with a 5-year-old needs to know there will be other 5-year-olds.

**Why people are coming** — word cloud or category breakdown: remote work freedom / family space / farming interest / slower pace / cost of living / community belonging. Reveals vibe more efficiently than any description.

**Skills and professions forming** — designers, engineers, teachers, farmers, caregivers. Shows if there will be interesting neighbors and whether the economic ecosystem makes sense.

**Nationality / background mix** — relevant for international families and returnees. A town with 30% foreign residents is a very different proposition than one that is 100% Japanese.

**Commitment velocity** — not just how many, but how fast. "12 families in the last 30 days" signals momentum and social proof.

**Intention alignment** — what the cohort wants to build together. If 60% of committed families have kids under 10 and flagged "want to start a food co-op," that's a specific gravitational pull. Someone who wants that will feel it immediately.

### The self-selection mechanism
The Oak House charts help people rule themselves *out* as much as in. Musubu should lean into this deliberately. A town attracting mostly 30s–40s families looks different from one drawing 20s remote workers — and both should be legible at a glance. Honest self-selection protects community fit long-term.

## Cold Start Problem — How Direction-Setting Solves It

A composition chart with 4 data points isn't meaningful. The declared direction fills that gap:

1. Town sets its direction at onboarding → initial vibe signal exists immediately
2. Survey respondents are matched against that direction → early interest accumulates
3. Real cohort data begins to fill in and refine the signal
4. Town's declared direction and actual incoming profile converge (or productively diverge)

**Threshold for showing live composition data:** Consider showing declared direction + "X families have expressed interest" until a meaningful threshold (e.g., 15–20 profiles) exists to display meaningfully.

## Clustering Effect — Platform-Level Intelligence

Once 30–50 towns have direction profiles and real interest data, natural clusters emerge. This enables:

- **Discovery by vibe** — families browse "towns like what you're looking for" rather than by prefecture
- **Infrastructure playbooks** — towns attracting the same segment get shared resources (co-working playbooks, broadband subsidy templates, school partnership models)
- **Company outreach by cluster** — Musubu can approach employers with "here are five towns all attracting tech-leaning remote workers" as a package
- **Government targeting** — subsidies directed at clusters with proven momentum rather than scattered interventions

**The platform learns what to build by watching where intention pools.** That's coordination working at a meta level — shaping Musubu's own roadmap based on where gravity forms naturally.

## Data Moat Principle

The survey isn't a one-time research exercise. It's a continuously improving intelligence layer.

- More towns join → survey base grows → data gets richer
- More families move → behavioral data improves → predictions sharpen
- More post-move reports → tipping point intelligence improves → towns convert better

Each cycle makes the platform more valuable to every participant. That's a genuine data moat — and the core argument for why Musubu becomes harder to replicate over time.

## Open Questions for Future Development

1. **Fit score methodology** — self-assessed by town, calculated by Musubu from survey data, or a combination?
2. **Direction update cadence** — one-time onboarding or regularly revisited? What triggers a review?
3. **Composition visibility threshold** — how many data points before live charts are shown publicly?
4. **Segment weighting** — should towns be able to signal which segments they most want to attract, or is that too curated?
5. **Divergence handling** — when declared direction and actual interest diverge significantly, who initiates the conversation and how?
6. **Post-move data collection** — opt-in survey, passive platform behavior, or structured check-ins at 3 / 6 / 12 months?
