# Famichi Pilot Town Analysis
## Strategic Assessment of Candidate Towns for First Launch

---

## Executive Summary

After analyzing 32+ Japanese towns needing repopulation, **three distinct tiers** of pilot candidates emerge. Our recommendation: Launch with **Bungotakada City** as the first Family Growth Town, followed by **Namie Town** as the first Tech Training Village, with **Imabari City** as a potential third-wave regional hub model.

**Key Insight:** The best pilot towns aren't necessarily the most desperate—they're the ones where **existing momentum + government support + infrastructure** create conditions for rapid, visible success that can be replicated.

---

## TIER 1: Primary Pilot Recommendation

### **Bungotakada City, Oita Prefecture**

**Population:** 22,500  
**Model:** Family Growth Town (Eldercare Focus)  
**Risk Level:** LOW  
**Impact Potential:** VERY HIGH  
**Timeline to Launch:** 12-18 months

#### Why Bungotakada is THE First Choice

**1. Proven Government Support (Critical)**
- Ranked #1 in under-30K category for livability
- Active family-friendly policies **already showing results**
- Track record of innovation (Showa Era tourism strategy)
- Local government demonstrates willingness to experiment
- Existing subsidy programs we can build on

**2. Perfect Population Size**
- 22,500 = Large enough for infrastructure
- 50 families = 2.2% population increase (highly visible impact)
- Existing schools, healthcare, services
- Not so small that we overwhelm the community
- Not so large that our impact gets lost

**3. Existing Momentum**
- Already attracting young families
- Remote workers moving in
- Community receptive to newcomers
- Success stories prove it's possible
- Positive narrative to build on

**4. Family-First Infrastructure**
- Comprehensive childcare support already in place
- Free medical care for children (government subsidized)
- Subsidized housing for families
- Intergenerational programs (elderly + newcomers)
- Eldercare facilities exist and need workers

**5. Geographic Advantages**
- Kyushu location (warm climate, quality of life)
- Good access to larger cities (Oita City, Fukuoka)
- Rural character maintained
- Not geographically isolated
- Within 2 hours of major airport

**6. Economic Diversification Ready**
- Traditional agriculture (base economy)
- Family support services (growing sector)
- Tourism infrastructure (Showa Era theme)
- Multiple pathways for job creation
- Not dependent on single industry

**7. Cultural Assets**
- Showa Era tourism theme (unique differentiation)
- Traditional rural culture
- Authentic rather than manufactured
- Community identity strong
- Heritage preservation active

#### Strategic Fit for Famichi Model

**Eldercare Demand:** ✅ Aging population = guaranteed job market  
**Training Infrastructure:** ✅ Can establish training center  
**Childcare Need:** ✅ Young families arriving = demand for co-op  
**Housing Stock:** ✅ Akiya available for renovation  
**Government Partnership:** ✅ Proven cooperation  
**Community Acceptance:** ✅ Already welcoming newcomers  

#### Launch Advantages

**Speed to Market:**
- Government support = faster permitting
- Existing programs = frameworks to build on
- Community acceptance = less resistance
- Infrastructure exists = lower upfront investment

**Proof of Concept:**
- Success here proves model works for thousands of similar towns
- National attention (smaller town = bigger story)
- Replicable scale (not too large, not too small)
- Clear before/after metrics

**Risk Mitigation:**
- Government won't block us (they want this)
- Community won't reject us (they're already welcoming)
- Infrastructure won't fail (it exists)
- Economic model viable (eldercare guaranteed)

#### Potential Challenges

**Challenge 1: Showa Era Tourism Conflicts**
- Risk: Theme park effect vs. authentic community
- Mitigation: Position Famichi as "living community" not tourist attraction
- Opportunity: Families moving in enhance authenticity

**Challenge 2: Small Scale Limits Growth**
- Risk: Can only absorb 50-100 families max
- Mitigation: This is PILOT—success leads to other towns
- Opportunity: Perfect size to prove model works

**Challenge 3: Rural Mindset**
- Risk: Traditional conservative culture
- Mitigation: They're already innovating (tourism, family policies)
- Opportunity: Modern families + traditional values = Famichi vision

#### Recommended Approach

**Phase 1: Partnership Building (Months 0-3)**
- Official visit with mayor and council
- Present Famichi model tailored to Bungotakada
- Identify local eldercare facility partners
- Secure government subsidy commitments
- Identify community champions

**Phase 2: Infrastructure Planning (Months 3-6)**
- Identify 10-15 akiya for renovation
- Plan childcare co-op location
- Establish training center partnerships
- Design community integration program
- Develop Showa Era integration strategy

**Phase 3: Recruitment & Community Building (Months 6-12)**
- Launch national survey with Bungotakada-specific questions
- Host trial "Live Like Local" visits
- Build online community of interested families
- Reach commitment threshold (50 families)
- Finalize move-in timeline

**Phase 4: Launch (Months 12-18)**
- Synchronized migration event
- Welcome ceremony and integration
- Begin eldercare training program
- Launch co-operatives
- Document and share story nationally

## TIER 1: Secondary Pilot Recommendation

### **Namie Town, Fukushima Prefecture**

**Population:** 21,900  
**Model:** Tech Training Village  
**Risk Level:** MEDIUM  
**Impact Potential:** VERY HIGH  
**Timeline to Launch:** 18-24 months

#### Why Namie as Second Launch

**1. Post-Disaster Innovation Opportunity**
- Government committed to rebuilding with innovation
- Modern infrastructure (recently built)
- National attention and support
- "Phoenix from the ashes" narrative
- Proof that even disaster areas can revive

**2. Sustainability & Innovation Focus**
- Already investing in green technology
- Smart city infrastructure
- Innovation mindset established
- Government grants available
- International interest

**3. Tech Training Perfect Fit**
- Need to attract young workers
- Modern infrastructure supports remote work
- Fiber internet guaranteed (part of rebuild)
- Government desperate to show success
- National pride at stake

**4. Complementary to Bungotakada**
- Different model (tech vs eldercare)
- Different region (Tohoku vs Kyushu)
- Different narrative (innovation vs tradition)
- Different demographics (younger families)
- Proves model diversity

#### Strategic Advantages

**National Attention:** Fukushima recovery = major news story  
**Government Support:** Unlimited (national priority)  
**Infrastructure:** Brand new (earthquake-resistant, modern)  
**Fiber Internet:** Guaranteed (already installed)  
**Innovation Culture:** Established (sustainability focus)  

#### Potential Challenges

**Challenge 1: Disaster Stigma**
- Risk: Radiation fears (mostly unfounded but persist)
- Mitigation: Transparent data, health monitoring, insurance
- Opportunity: Overcome stigma = powerful story

**Challenge 2: Psychological Burden**
- Risk: Trauma in community, heavy history
- Mitigation: Focus on future, not past; new residents bring optimism
- Opportunity: Healing through renewal

**Challenge 3: Over-Reliance on Government**
- Risk: Government funding could disappear
- Mitigation: Build sustainable economic model
- Opportunity: Use funding to establish, then sustain independently

#### Recommended Timeline

**Why Second (Not First):**
- Prove Famichi model works first in easier environment
- Build credibility before tackling higher-risk town
- Learn from Bungotakada before Namie
- Namie deserves our best effort = need experience first

**Launch Timing:**
- Start Bungotakada Month 0
- Begin Namie groundwork Month 6
- Launch Namie Month 18-24
- Both running simultaneously Year 3+

## TIER 2: Regional Hub Model

### **Imabari City, Ehime Prefecture**

**Population:** 158,000 (includes rural districts)  
**Model:** Regional Hub / Multiple Models  
**Risk Level:** MEDIUM-HIGH  
**Impact Potential:** VERY HIGH (if successful)  
**Timeline to Launch:** 24-36 months

#### Why Imabari as Third Wave

**1. Different Scale Entirely**
- Tests whether Famichi works in mid-sized cities
- Could impact hundreds of thousands if replicated
- Regional economic center (not just rural)
- Maritime industry modernization opportunity
- Multiple models simultaneously possible

**2. Startup-Friendly Environment**
- Ranked #1 in 100K-200K category
- Visa programs for entrepreneurs
- Business support infrastructure
- Tech sector development underway
- International orientation

**3. Island Communities Within**
- Rural islands desperately need revival
- City acts as support hub
- Test urban-rural coordination
- Maritime heritage unique angle
- Multiple mini-pilots possible

**4. Manufacturing Potential**
- Traditional maritime industry
- Companies could relocate facilities
- Skilled trades workforce needed
- Manufacturing Town model viable
- Economic diversification ready

#### Strategic Advantages

**Scale Testing:** Proves model works beyond small towns  
**Infrastructure:** Extensive (city-level services)  
**Economic Diversity:** Multiple sectors to partner with  
**International:** Already attracting foreign entrepreneurs  
**Replication Impact:** Success here = model for hundreds of cities  

#### Potential Challenges

**Challenge 1: Scale Complexity**
- Risk: Too large to see clear Famichi impact
- Mitigation: Focus on specific districts/islands
- Opportunity: Multiple cohorts simultaneously

**Challenge 2: Bureaucratic Complexity**
- Risk: Larger government = slower decisions
- Mitigation: Focus on island sub-units with autonomy
- Opportunity: More resources available

**Challenge 3: Less Urgent Need**
- Risk: City will survive without us
- Mitigation: Frame as "regional hub model" not rescue
- Opportunity: Less desperate = less pressure

#### Recommended Approach

**Why Third (Not First or Second):**
- Too complex for first pilot
- Need to prove small-town model first
- Requires more sophisticated execution
- Different value proposition (innovation hub vs rescue)

**Strategic Positioning:**
- Launch after Bungotakada and Namie show success
- Frame as "scaling up" not "starting from scratch"
- Partner with existing startup ecosystem
- Focus on island communities within city boundaries
- Test multiple town types simultaneously

## Strategic Recommendations

### Recommended Launch Sequence

**Year 1: Bungotakada (Family Growth Town)**
- Lowest risk
- Highest replication potential
- Proves basic Famichi model works
- Builds credibility
- Establishes co-op frameworks
- Creates media attention

**Year 2: Namie (Tech Training Village)**
- Different model (proves diversity)
- Different region (proves geography irrelevant)
- National significance (Fukushima recovery)
- Tech talent narrative
- Government backing unlimited
- International attention

**Year 3: Imabari (Regional Hub)**
- Scale test (mid-sized city model)
- Multiple models simultaneously
- Economic diversity
- International orientation
- Proves model works beyond small towns
- Regional impact potential

**Years 4-5: Specialized Towns**
- Yuzawa (second Tech Training)
- Kotoura (Agricultural Innovation)
- Shirakawa (Creative Hub/Heritage)
- Multiple launches simultaneously
- Proven playbook by this point
- Network effects across towns

### Critical Success Factors

**For ALL Towns:**
1. ✅ Government partnership secured BEFORE launch
2. ✅ Community champions identified and engaged
3. ✅ Infrastructure exists or funding secured
4. ✅ Economic model validated (jobs guaranteed)
5. ✅ Housing stock identified (10-15+ akiya)
6. ✅ Fiber internet confirmed or planned
7. ✅ Within 2 hours of major city
8. ✅ Eldercare/tech/agricultural facilities exist

**For Bungotakada Specifically:**
1. ✅ Eldercare facility partnerships (3+ facilities)
2. ✅ Training center location secured
3. ✅ Childcare co-op space identified
4. ✅ Government subsidy commitments in writing
5. ✅ Mayor and council full support
6. ✅ Showa Era theme integration strategy
7. ✅ Community integration plan developed
8. ✅ Local media partnership established

### Why NOT Other Towns (For First Pilot)

**Too Small (Under 3,000):**
- Hinoemata (2,400), Kamikoani (2,800), Higashinaruse (2,900)
- Risk of overwhelming existing community
- Insufficient infrastructure
- Better as second-wave after proving model

**Too Isolated:**
- Mountain villages with limited access
- Emergency services concerns
- Difficult for trial visits
- Limits economic opportunities

**Insufficient Government Support:**
- No demonstrated track record
- Unknown if they'd cooperate
- Risk of bureaucratic resistance
- First pilot must be slam dunk

**Wrong Economic Model:**
- Towns without eldercare, tech, or agricultural base
- Would require building economy from scratch
- Too risky for first pilot
- Better to build on existing foundations

2. **Partnership proposal** - Present Famichi model tailored to their needs
3. **Facility partnerships** - Secure eldercare facility commitments
4. **Housing survey** - Identify available akiya for renovation

**Short-term (3-12 Months):**

3. **Infrastructure planning** - Finalize locations for childcare, training, housing
4. **Economic partnerships** - Secure training program funding, company hiring commitments
5. **Commitment threshold** - Reach 50 families ready to move

**Medium-term (12-24 Months):**
1. **Bungotakada launch** - First 50 families move
2. **Namie groundwork** - Begin same process for second town
3. **Documentation** - Capture lessons learned, refine playbook
4. **National attention** - Media coverage, government recognition
5. **Replication planning** - Identify towns 3-5

**The Recommendation:**

**Start with Bungotakada. Prove it works. Scale from there.**

Every great movement starts with one bold step. Bungotakada is that step—the perfect combination of readiness, need, and impact potential. Success here unlocks hundreds of other towns. Failure would set us back years.

But with their proven government support, existing momentum, and family-friendly culture, **Bungotakada isn't risky—it's ready.**
