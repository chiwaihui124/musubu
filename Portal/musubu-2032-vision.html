Why the numbers are small

176 households over six years is not a boom. It's roughly four hundred people in a town of twenty-two thousand — under two percent. That is deliberately what success looks like here: enough arrivals, concentrated closely enough in time and place, to reopen a kindergarten and make a café viable. A picture showing the town doubling would be a fantasy, and anyone who knows these towns would stop reading.

What's still shown as failed

Three businesses opened and closed. Twelve percent of arrived households left again. One housing cluster never reached its threshold and the land is still paddy. These are on the map on purpose — a version of the future with no failures in it is marketing, and the whole argument of this project is that the honest version is more persuasive than the polished one.
