The mechanism

The choice was never Tokyo versus the countryside.
It was Tokyo versus a question mark.

You cannot evaluate a town you have never lived in. Not the work, not the rent, not whether you would be the only one there. A place nobody can assess is not an option — it is a guess.

So here is the whole idea in one screen. Watch what happens to the unknowns as people commit.

Nothing has happened yet. Nobody has moved, nobody has paid, nobody has given notice on a flat. Every commitment here is conditional — it only becomes real if the town fills.

What coordination actually adds

Not persuasion. Specification. Committing people together is what makes a place describable in advance — who is coming, what the work pays, when it happens, what the rent is, and what occurs if it does not fill.

That is the whole trick. It borrows the conditional-pledge mechanic that already works elsewhere and points it at the one thing you cannot buy on your own: arriving somewhere at the same moment as everyone else, where nobody is already inside.

And if it does not fill, nothing happens. Nobody is left having moved alone. That is what makes committing early cheap enough to be worth doing.
