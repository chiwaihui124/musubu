The thing nobody could find

Five corpus documents were absent from the README entirely. Three of them were written on the same day, 9 August, and together they are a complete and immediately actionable first move on a town nobody has been talking about.

  • musubu_tokyo_corridor_candidates scores towns on an axis the main pilot analysis never used: how far the town is from the people you are trying to move. It surfaces 奥多摩町 Okutama and 檜原村 Hinohara, both inside Tokyo Prefecture, through an eligibility door the document says nobody uses.
  • musubu_success_thresholds reverse-engineers what success would actually mean for Okutama from the town's own arithmetic, and lands a finding that should change recruitment: cohorts must be age-clustered, not merely large. A school does not reopen because forty people arrived. It reopens because enough of them have six-year-olds. That qualifies the fifty-family threshold used everywhere else in the corpus.
  • musubu_first_contact_drafts contains outreach drafted in Japanese, with a section headed what is actually sendable today, aimed at Okutama on the school roll and at ふるさと回帰支援センター.

A scored candidate town, the arithmetic for what would count as success there, and Japanese-language letters ready to send. All three have been sitting in the folder since 9 August, and none of them was reachable from the index everyone was told to start at. That is the whole argument for doing this, and it is a better one than any structure I could have proposed.

Sixty open threads

Every unresolved question in the corpus, pulled into one list and tagged by what would actually close it. That turns out to be more useful than a status: a question nobody can answer yet is a completely different thing from one waiting on a decision somebody could make this afternoon.

The distribution is the finding. 24 threads are decisions that need no new information at all. 9 need a Japanese speaker, which is the project's known structural gap and the clearest evidence yet for the team-first strategy. 4 need a lawyer. 10 cannot move until something is actually running, which is the real cost of nothing being built.

What changed today

  • Five documents indexed for the first time, including the Okutama cluster above. The README now covers all 51.
  • Two orphans promoted into the corpus. musubu_akiya_strategy (3,555 words on the apprenticeship economy and akiya conversion) and musubu_implementation_roadmap (the only end-to-end sequence on file). Both had sat in the project root with no home.
  • Sixteen superseded Famichi-era files archived to _archive/famichi-era/ with a mapping table. Nothing deleted. The project root is now two files instead of eighteen.
  • Twelve Portal pages were missing a charset declaration and rendered Japanese as mojibake. Nine needed the fix and got it; three already had one properly placed and were left alone.
  • Everything outside the corpus folder is now indexed: the fifteen Portal prototypes, the four external syntheses in Gemini Notebook/, and the eighteen loose research files that make up most of the Ground branch.
  • OPEN_THREADS.md created as a standing instrument alongside the claims register.

What is still worth doing

The population-tier research in particular is the ground under musubu_business_coordination, which currently asserts its service thresholds without pointing anywhere.
  • Lift the comparison table out of the Gemini synthesis. Musubu against Kickstarter, participatory budgeting, Decidim, Loomio, community wealth building and civic asset mapping. It exists nowhere in the corpus and belongs in musubu_precedent_research.
  • Twenty-four threads are decisions waiting on nobody. Filter the list above by DECIDE and a real number of them could close in an afternoon.
