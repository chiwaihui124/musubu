You're deciding between staying put and going to Tokyo.
There's a third option.

Twenty people around your age moving to the same small town in the same season. Not to retire. To work, learn something, and have a life that costs a fifth of what Tokyo charges for a worse version of it.

The question you actually care about

Who else is going.

Nobody moves unless all twenty do. You can see who's in before you decide, and they can see you. This is the entire point — arriving alone into a small town is a genuinely bad idea and you'd be right to refuse it.

What it costs you — said properly, not in a footnote

This is worse than Tokyo at several things.

The dating pool is small

Twenty people your age is twenty people. It is not a city. Some couples form and some people find it lonely in a way the money does not fix.

There is no career ladder here

If you want to be a consultant at a big firm, this is the wrong move and we would rather you knew now.

Not much happens on a Wednesday

No gigs most nights, no galleries, nothing open at 3am. You will make your own things to do or you will be bored.

You will be visible

Everyone will know who you are and roughly what you did last weekend. For some people that is the appeal and for others it is unbearable.

People will ask why you left

Some of them will assume you failed at something. That is their problem, but you will have to hear it at every New Year for a while.

The town is not a blank slate

Around 5,000 people already live there and did not ask for you. Most will be glad. Some will not. Both are reasonable.

And the thing that makes it not a gamble

You can leave. That's designed in, not a loophole.

1

Come for a weekend first

Free. You will meet most of them at Friday dinner whether you plan to or not.

2

Then nine months, not forever

Paid work, room included, and the decision about staying happens at month seven — not now.

3

If it's too small, move up rather than back

There's a town of 22,000 forty minutes away with a hospital, high schools and more going on. Leaving the village does not mean going back to Tokyo.

4

If nobody comes, nothing happens

Under twenty and the whole thing is called off. Deposits back. You will not be the person who moved and then watched everyone else pull out.

Twenty people, one season, one town.

Being 23 is the cheapest time in your life to try something — no mortgage, no school year to work around, nobody's career but your own. That is not a reason to be reckless. It is a reason to spend it on something you would actually learn from.

Not 23

Three other doors into the same thing.

30s

You are not starting over. You are taking skills somewhere they count for more, with your housing cost cut in half and a reason to be somewhere.

50s

Retired

Arriving with others your age, near a clinic, with people who need what you know. The thing most people miss is not care, it is being needed.
